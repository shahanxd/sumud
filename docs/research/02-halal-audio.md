# halal-audio

## Summary
Research for SUMUD's halal audio and scene design, from pages actually opened (islamqa.info, islamweb.net, seekersguidance.org, dar-alifta.org, Darul Ifta Birmingham, FatwaCentre, IslamOnline, MuslimMatters, IQNA, Middle East Eye, We Are Not Numbers, Al Jazeera, Globe and Mail, MEMO, New Arab, CS Monitor, HRW, Raseef22, Times of Israel, Wikipedia, Apple Music, Bandcamp). Headline positions: (1) Instruments: the four madhhabs' relied-upon position (islamqa, seekersguidance Hanafi and Shafi'i answers, islamweb, Darul Ifta Birmingham) prohibits all instruments except the duff frame drum, which is permitted at weddings, Eid and the return of a traveller; islamqa restricts the duff to women, seekersguidance's Hanafi answer says percussion is "generally acceptable" but recommends sticking to the duff; Egypt's Dar al-Ifta is the mainstream permissive outlier, allowing instruments with content conditions. Vocals, clapping (debated: Ibn Baz/Standing Committee say makruh-to-haram, IslamOnline scholars permit it), natural sounds and mechanical sounds (islamqa explicitly lists water, wind, animals, artillery, vehicles, breaking glass as permitted) are fine; beatboxing and any vocal or synthesised sound that imitates an instrument is prohibited by islamqa, FatwaCentre and islamweb ("what counts is what reaches the ears"), permitted by Darul Ifta Birmingham only if unprocessed mouth sound. (2) Vocals-only nasheed is an established commercial format: Maher Zain's "Thank You Allah (Vocals Only - No Music Version)" (Awakening, 9 Feb 2012, 17 tracks), Ahmed Bukhatir (no instruments across his whole career), Native Deen (drums and synths only, no wind/string/metal), Zain Bhikha (daf/djembe and backing vocalists), Nasheed Bay (instrument-free stance). Film precedent: ROSE (Berlinale 2026, Tara Nome Doyle) has an entirely a cappella score and won the Diagonale best-score prize; Incredibox (2M+ sales) is a game whose entire sound is a cappella voice. No game with an original all-vocal narrative score was found. (3) Quran etiquette: Hanafi view says attentive listening is obligatory, majority says recommended; Ibn Baz permits playing recordings if no idle talk or argument happens over them; islamweb says listening while playing a game is worse than stopping and one should switch the player off only at the end of a verse or surah (this rule does not apply to nasheeds); islamqa permits verses over video only if the scene matches the verse's earthly meaning, with no music and, in its strict view, no women depicted; Quran on a screen is not a mushaf (no wudu needed); Al-Azhar and Dar al-Ifta jointly declared Quran-with-music clips prohibited (Sept 2024). (4) AI: islamqa permits AI recitation only with the voice owner's permission and for permissible purposes; auto-tune on Quran is not permitted; MUI (Indonesia, Fatwa 30/2023) and Dar al-Ifta (18 Jan 2026, reported second-hand) allow synthetic recitation only as a reviewed learning tool, not as tilawah; a Hanafi darul ifta permits ordinary AI voiceovers. (5) Gazan weddings: multi-day, gender-separated (women's henna night with songs, duff and zaghareet; men's sahra; groom's public shaving; zaffa; men's dabke to Dal'ona/Zareef al-Tool; brides celebrate indoors separately even at Hamas-affiliated mass weddings); wartime weddings moved to tents and UNRWA school shelters with singing and "wedding drums", and by 2026 separate men's and women's celebrations have become hard to hold. Traditional zaffa and dabke use tabl, mizmar, mijwiz and tablah, so a halal version must substitute voices, duff and clapping, which the wartime context makes authentic. (6) Depiction: Gaza school custom (not law) requires a white headscarf and abaya/jilbab from the preparatory and secondary stages, so a 12-year-old is at the threshold and would plausibly be covered outdoors; animated women are acceptable to islamweb if not scantily clad and not paired with music; islamqa's strict view rejects women dancing even before women, while islamweb allows dancing at lawful weddings with conditions; a recorded adult woman singing to a mixed audience is a condition-violation in the strict view (islamqa 91142) and "not before unrelated males" even for Dar al-Ifta, which makes Teta's sung lullaby the highest-risk item in the current audio plan.

## Findings
- [verified] Majority classical position: all instruments are prohibited except the duff (frame drum), which is permitted on specific joyful occasions.
  islamqa 222730 states singing with instruments is prohibited by the four schools and cites Ibn Hajar al-Haytami: stringed instruments 'are all prohibited, and there is no difference of scholarly opinion regarding that'. islamqa 152009 explains the duff exception acoustically: 'The daff is the least stirring of all musical instruments, hence sharee'ah has permitted it in some cases', mainly to announce weddings. islamqa 20406 limits permitted occasions to Eid, weddings and the return of a traveller (some scholars add births and circumcisions) and cites the hadith of the girls beating the daff at Mina ('Leave them alone, O Abu Bakr, for these are the days of Eid'), the wedding hadith ('our little girls started beating the daff'), and the woman who vowed to beat the daff on the Prophet's return. islamweb 339946 says 'there are texts regarding the prohibition of all musical instruments with the exception of the Duff' and that Shafi'i jurists allow small children's drums 'like the Duff, provided that they are not like big drums'. SeekersGuidance's Shafi'i answer (Shaykh Irshaad Sedick): 'According to most Scholars, the Duff Drum is excluded from the general prohibition', while lute, oud and cymbals are haram. Darul Ifta Birmingham: only the daff, 'used at weddings for women only'.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/222730; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/152009; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/20406; https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/339946/ruling-on-percussions-other-than-duff; https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/what-is-the-shafii-ruling-on-listening-to-music-and-singing/; https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/beatboxing-musical-instruments-and-maqaamat/

- [verified] Who may beat the duff is itself disputed: islamqa says women only; the Hanafi answer on SeekersGuidance says percussion is generally acceptable but recommends the duff.
  islamqa 20406: 'Only women are permitted' to beat the daff; men doing so is imitating women, citing 'Clapping is for women, and Tasbeeh is for men' and the curse on men who imitate women. SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Abdul-Rahim Reasat, checked by Faraz Rabbani): 'In the Hanafi school, percussion instruments are generally acceptable', 'Most scholars agree that the frame drum (daff) is permissible', and 'It's probably safer to stick to a daff'. SeekersGuidance (Shaykh Yusuf Weltch): the duff was used 'sometimes even in His presence without His objection'; Hanafis differ on whether it is restricted to weddings; minority scholars such as 'Abd al-Ghani al-Nabulsi took a relaxed view of spiritual music. Practical consequence for SUMUD: a duff on the women's side of the wedding is safe under every view; a duff in the men's dabke is safe under Hanafi/permissive views and objectionable under islamqa's view.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/20406; https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hanafi-fiqh/is-it-permissible-to-play-musical-instruments/; https://seekersguidance.org/answers/halal-and-haram/is-listening-to-music-haram/

- [verified] Egypt's Dar al-Ifta is the mainstream permissive position: instruments and singing are permissible with content conditions.
  Dar al-Ifta fatwa 4866: 'singing, whether with musical instruments or without, is permissible with the condition that it not a call for disobedience'; conditions include no lewd content, no excess, women should not perform before unrelated males, and intention matters. Fatwa 6870 lists Ibn Hazm, al-Ghazali, Izz al-Din ibn Abd al-Salam and various Shafi'i and Hanafi scholars as permitting instruments, quotes al-Ghazali ('Entertainment is the medicine of the heart against the disease of weariness') and says drums at weddings 'are like beating the duff... permissible on such occasions if accompanied with songs containing good words'. SeekersGuidance's Shafi'i answer names al-Ghazali and al-Qaradawi as the permitting minority. Because the founder has chosen the strict standard, the game should follow the majority view; the Dar al-Ifta view is worth knowing so the team can explain to players why the strict choice is a choice, not the only Islam.
  src: https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/4866/what-is-the-ruling-concerning-music; https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/6870/musical-instruments-in-islam; https://seekersguidance.org/answers/shafii-fiqh/what-is-the-shafii-ruling-on-listening-to-music-and-singing/

- [verified] Beatboxing and vocal percussion are debated; the decisive test in most fatwas is whether the sound imitates an instrument.
  Prohibiting: islamqa 193426 'In our opinion it is prohibited because it sounds like, and could be taking the place of, musical instruments', while natural human sounds (laughter, crying) and sounds like water or animals are fine; FatwaCentre (Hanafi, checked by Mufti Abdul Rahman Mangera and Mufti Zubair Patel): 'If the sounds being produced just resemble sounds from musical instruments then we would consider that to be impermissible'. Permitting with limits: Darul Ifta Birmingham 'As long as it comes from the mouth it is permissible', but not if processed to resemble instruments; islamweb 212779 permits vocal effects 'like echoes, sounds of water and birds' and repeating or extending the voice, but 'what counts is what reaches the ears', so anything heard as instrumental music is prohibited. Maqamat (the modal system) are explicitly permissible (Darul Ifta Birmingham), so maqam-based sung melodies are fine.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/193426; https://islamqa.org/hanafi/fatwacentre/270660/vocal-and-beatboxing/; https://daruliftabirmingham.co.uk/home/beatboxing-musical-instruments-and-maqaamat/; https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/212779/using-beatboxing-in-islamic-songs-and-dawah-clips

- [verified] Synthesised drones or computer rhythms that sound like instruments carry the same ruling as instruments; natural and mechanical sounds, including artillery, are explicitly permitted.
  islamqa 229732: 'whatever resembles something wrong is also wrong, and Islamic teachings do not differentiate between things that are similar'; vocal, beatbox and electronic effects that mimic instruments are prohibited regardless of production method. The same answer lists permitted sounds: water, wind, animals, human weeping and laughing, and 'mechanical sounds (artillery, vehicles, breaking glass)', with the caveat that such sounds 'should not accompany Qur'anic recitation'. islamqa 107570: computer rhythms in nasheeds are haram because they 'sound no different to music', unless they genuinely sound identical to a daff in a permissible context. islamqa 91142 sets eight conditions for a permissible nasheed, including no instruments, no 'sound effects that imitate musical instruments', 'the purpose of the nasheed should be the words, not the tune', and the performer 'should not be a woman performing in front of men'. Consequence: SUMUD's wind, sea, rumble and explosion design is halal-neutral; the planned oud/qanun/ney/darbuka score and any synth pad imitating them must go; vocal-only textures (breath, humming, held vowels) are the safe replacement as long as they do not read as a synth or string pad.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/229732; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/107570; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/91142

- [verified] Clapping at celebrations is debated: the Saudi strict view calls it makruh at best, other mainstream scholars permit it outside prayer.
  islamqa 20047 quotes Ibn Baz: clapping at parties resembles jahiliyyah, 'the least that can be said about it is that it is makruh', and evidence suggests haram; the Standing Committee applies this to weddings; the recommended alternative is saying 'Subhan Allah' or 'Allahu akbar'; the only exception is women clapping in prayer to alert the imam. IslamOnline (Dr Rif'at Fawzi, Cairo University; Sheikh Ahmad Kutty, Toronto): 'there is nothing in the sources of Shari'ah that prohibits it'; clapping is forbidden only in salah and is permitted at weddings as customary applause if not mixed with prohibited practices. Design consequence: clapping is culturally central to sahja and dabke and acceptable to most of the audience, but if the studio wants zero objections it can favour foot-stamping, zaghareet and call-and-response over hand claps in the men's scenes.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/20047; https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/is-clapping-permissible/

- [verified] Vocals-only nasheed is an established, commercially proven format with named artists and albums.
  Apple Music lists 'Thank You Allah (Vocals Only - No Music Version)' by Maher Zain, released 9 February 2012 on Awakening Worldwide, 17 tracks, 1h09m; Wikipedia records the original 2009 album at over 300,000 copies (8x platinum Malaysia, 20x platinum Indonesia). Wikipedia on Ahmed Bukhatir: 'Throughout his recording career he has continued to sing without musical instruments according to the Islamic Sharia law' (albums include Entasaf Al-Layl, Fartaqi, Samtan, Da'ani, Hasanat, Moments with Allah). Wikipedia on Native Deen: 'wind, string, and metal instruments are never used', only drums, synthesised sounds and vocals. Wikipedia on Zain Bhikha: 'sometimes performs with a djembe or daf and several backup vocalists' (the backing singers performed in The Lion King). Wikipedia's Nasheed article names Nasheed Bay as promoting 'an instrument-free stance'. Ulumalazhar's overview (secondary) describes traditional nasheed as 'usually performed a cappella... or with minimal percussion, mainly using the duff', with solo lead plus chorus, while modern productions use 'layered vocals, and intricate compositions'. Claims found only in search snippets (Zain Bhikha 'voice only' bonus tracks, Native Deen's 2012 voice-only album, the Popular Music & Society paper on Awakening's two versions) could not be opened and are excluded.

- [verified] There is award-winning precedent for an entirely a cappella dramatic score (film) and a best-selling all-voice game, but no verified game with an original all-vocal narrative score.
  Tara Nome Doyle's score for ROSE (dir. Markus Schleinzer, starring Sandra Hüller) is described on the composer's Bandcamp as 'No instruments, only the architecture of breath and tone'; it premiered in Competition at the 76th Berlinale, won the Silver Bear for Best Actress and the Diagonale Prize for Best Film Score, and IndieWire said it 'gives ROSE the hallowed feeling of something holy'. Incredibox (So Far So Good, first released 16 August 2009) is built entirely from 'a cappella sound loop[s]' performed by Paul Malburet and had reached 2 million app sales by January 2023. Searches for a narrative game whose original score is voice-only returned only a cappella cover projects (Video Game Music Choir, Peter Hollens), so that specific precedent is unverified.
  src: https://taranomedoyle.bandcamp.com/album/rose-original-motion-picture-score; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incredibox

- [verified] Quran as background audio: Hanafi view makes attentive listening obligatory; majority view makes it recommended; all views say do not let idle activity run over it.
  islamqa 88728: the Hanafi school holds that listening attentively outside prayer is obligatory (Quran 7:204), the majority hold it is recommended (Imam Ahmad: 'They were unanimously agreed that this applies to prayer'), and 'It is not good manners to ignore the Book of Allah when it is being recited, even if it is on a tape'. islamqa 174743 (Ibn Baz): leaving a recording playing is fine 'so long as' no idle talk or argument accompanies it; if people nearby argue or chat, 'it's better to turn off the broadcast'. SeekersGuidance (Faraz Rabbani): try 'one's reasonable best to pay attention', and 'avoid mentally busying yourself completely with other things (such as reading a book or article while listening to the Quran)'; when genuinely busy, play nasheeds instead. islamweb 86274: the Hanafi school says one 'has to give up playing while listening to the Qur'anic recitation', although listening while playing 'is better than not listening to it at all, or listening to something Haram'. Buruj Academy (secondary) classifies gaming during Quran as khilaf al-awla at best and 'lā yajūz' for loud games. Consequence: the founder's idea of 'Quran instead of music' as a bed is the one thing the scholarship is united against; Quran must be a full stop, nasheed is the bed.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/88728; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/174743/leaving-a-recording-of-the-quran-playing-without-listening-to-it; https://seekersguidance.org/answers/general-counsel/listening-to-quran-while-occupied-with-other-tasks/; https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/86274/hear-quran-while-playing; https://burujacademy.com/blog/can-i-listen-to-the-quran-while-playing-video-games/

- [verified] Interrupting recitation mid-verse is disliked and can distort meaning; stop only at the end of a verse or surah. This does not apply to nasheeds.
  islamweb 267796: 'the most valid opinion is that one should switch off the player at the end of a Soorah or verse', citing Ibn al-Jazari that the Companions 'disliked reciting a verse and stopping before completing it'; 'Listening to the recitation of the Quran is the same as reciting it in this regard'; and 'This restriction does not apply to audio poetry or religious songs (Anaasheed)'. islamqa 229818: pausing at verse ends is Sunnah (Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Taymiyah, Umm Salamah hadith), but 'it is disallowed to stop reciting altogether at a particular point if that will change the meaning' (example: stopping after 'So woe unto those performers of Salat'). islamweb 86698: a stop that 'gives a meaning other than that determined by Allah' is a grave sin; consult al-Nawawi's al-Tibyan for etiquette. Design consequence: the skip button must be disabled until the current ayah ends, and any cut list must be checked by someone who knows waqf rules.
  src: https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/267796/waiting-until-the-end-of-a-chapter-or-verse-to-switch-off-the-quran; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/229818/pausing-at-the-ends-of-verses-is-sunnah-even-if-they-are-connected-to-what-follows; https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/86698/stopping-while-reciting-quran

- [verified] Placing Quran verses over visual scenes is permitted by islamqa if the scene matches the verse's worldly meaning, with no music and, in its strict view, no women on screen; verses about the unseen must not be paired with worldly imagery.
  islamqa 175174: 'We do not think that there is anything wrong with doing that because it is no more than a scene that is in harmony with the meaning of the verses'; guidelines are no musical accompaniment, no depiction of women, and verses matched to observable phenomena (sea, mountains, animals, honouring parents, helping orphans); showing real fire and quoting Hell verses is rejected because 'they have nothing in common but names'. Al-Azhar and Dar al-Ifta (joint warning, 22 September 2024, reported by IQNA): 'creating and broadcasting clips of Quran recitations accompanied by music is prohibited, as it is considered disrespectful to the Holy Quran'; the clips concerned were AI-generated. islamqa 229732 adds that even permitted natural sounds should not accompany recitation. islamqa 106961: Quran on a screen 'cannot be called a Mus-haf' and may be read or touched without wudu, so on-screen verse display raises no purity issue.
  src: https://islamqa.info/en/answers/175174; https://iqna.ir/en/news/3489989/egypt%E2%80%99s-al-azhar-dar-al-ifta-issue-warning-against-quran-recitations-with-music; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/229732; https://islamqa.info/en/answers/106961

- [verified] AI-generated Quran recitation is permitted by islamqa only with the voice owner's permission and for permissible purposes; auto-tune on Quran is not permitted; institutional fatwas allow synthetic recitation only as a reviewed learning tool.
  islamqa 512399: 'It should be done with the permission of the owner of the voice', for permissible purposes, without harm; the voice owner gets no recitation reward but 'will be rewarded for benefitting people'; it warns that 'Someone may take a person's voice and manipulate it, making it say evil words'. islamqa 391117: auto-tune 'is not permissible' on Quran because it drops letters and tajwid; other effects are barred if they repeat sounds or make the reciter 'sound like the voice of a singer'. islamweb 392941: imitating a famous reciter's style is acceptable to Ibn 'Uthaymeen 'if the person who imitates has a good voice and recites properly' but disliked by Bakr Abu Zayd. Indonesian MUI Fatwa No. 30 (2023), reported by GlobalPlanet (Indonesian, secondary): AI recitation 'cannot substitute for tilawah' but is mubah as a tajweed learning tool; AI adhan is invalid. MuslimMatters (28 Sept 2026, secondary) reports that on 18 January 2026 Egypt's Dar al-Ifta 'permitted AI-assisted electronic Qur'anic tools and synthetic recitations for legitimate educational purposes, provided that qualified bodies review them for accuracy'; the primary Dar al-Ifta page was not found. Darul Fiqh (Mufti Faraz Adam): ordinary AI voiceovers for educational videos are permissible because they are 'automated reproduction rather than genuine speech'. Consequence: ElevenLabs is acceptable for character dialogue; cloning Mishary Alafasy, Israr Ahmed or any reciter without written permission is out; use licensed human recordings for every Quran moment.

- [verified] A Gazan wedding is multi-day and gender-separated: women's henna night with songs, duff-style drumming and zaghareet; a men's sahra; the groom's public shaving; a zaffa procession; men's dabke and sahja call-and-response.
  We Are Not Numbers (a Gazan writer): 'All the women gather and sing traditional Arabic songs' at the henna night; the groom hosts a men's 'sahra'; 'All the women wear their thoub'; 'The nights are full of zaghareet'; wedding day has the groom's public shaving, a zaffa 'with musicians and dancers', then dabke 'where guests stand in a line holding hands... kicking their legs into the air'. Middle East Eye: henna night is women-only; sahjih is a men's line dance where 'one singer will call out a verse, lauding the groom, and the guests and other dancers will respond'; zaghrouta is the women's ululation. Wikipedia Music of Palestine: 'the zajal leads the sahja with a verse, and the entire group repeats'; sahja is performed by men or by women; a woman starts the zaghroot with 'Heeey Hee...' or 'Aweeha...'. Wikipedia Dabke: Dal'ona, Al Jafra and Zareef il-Tool are 'entire genres' where lyrics vary but rhythm is fixed; Al-Daḥiyya is men-only, Al-Ḥashi women-only. Falastin: the bride's tatreez thobe identifies her village; weddings run four to seven days. Wedded Wonderland: the tulbe and jaha (formal request for the bride).

- [verified] Real Gazan zaffa and dabke use instruments the strict view forbids (tabl, mizmar, mijwiz, tablah, shubbabah, yarghool), so a halal wedding scene must consciously substitute voice, duff and clapping; the wartime setting makes that substitution authentic.
  Wedded Wonderland: the zaffe is led by 'tabl (drums) and mizmar (wind instrument)'. Wikipedia Dabke: mijwiz (reed clarinet), tablah/durbakke, daff/riq. Wikipedia Music of Palestine: Dal'ona is sung 'along with the sound of the shubbabah (flute), yarghool or mijwiz'. Wartime reporting shows stripped-down weddings: New Arab (Nuseirat school shelter): displaced people 'sang traditional Palestinian patriotic and folk songs while wedding drums played' and carried the groom on their shoulders as aircraft flew overhead; Al Jazeera (Deir el-Balah camp, 16 Feb 2024): the bride sat with mother and sisters inside a tent 'while women outside sang traditional songs'; Globe and Mail: 'Men and women celebrate separately... the women stayed inside the house while the men sat outside', classical songs were played though 'few hear any more in Gaza', battery lights replaced electricity, no kunafa, no procession cars; Al Jazeera (12 Sept 2026): men's dabke and women's zaghrouta 'have largely disappeared', separate celebrations 'are no longer feasible', halls are destroyed or near the 'Yellow Line', couples marry in tents or mass weddings. So a Day 10 courtyard wedding with only voices, a duff on the women's side, clapping and stamping is both documented and halal.

## Recommendations
- Replace the maqam score's oud/qanun/ney/darbuka/piano/strings plan with a vocals-first score: male lead and male chorus in maqam (maqamat are explicitly permitted), held-vowel and breath textures as pads, duff only in women's wedding scenes and the Eid-like joy beats, zaghareet, foot-stamps and (optionally) claps for the men's dabke, and synthesised wind/sea/dust/rumble. Any synth pad must be audibly a human voice or a natural sound, never an imitation of a string, reed or drum, because islamqa, islamweb and FatwaCentre judge by 'what reaches the ears'.
- Do not use beatboxing that imitates drums or bass; if vocal percussion is needed, keep it to clearly vocal syllables and get a scholar's sign-off, since the rulings split (islamqa/FatwaCentre prohibit, Darul Ifta Birmingham permits unprocessed mouth sound).
- Keep explosion, artillery and vehicle sounds: islamqa 229732 lists them as permissible sound design. The decision to use a real explosion sound is a taste decision about the Inside-style aesthetic, not a fiqh one; the only halal constraint is that no such sound plays under Quran recitation.
- Implement Quran moments as a 'full stop' system: gameplay pauses, camera holds on a non-human subject (sea, sky, hands, the wall of names), no ambience, no SFX, no nasheed under it, one complete ayah or short passage with a licensed human reciter, Arabic text with translation and surah:ayah reference on screen, skip disabled until the current ayah ends (allow skip only at ayah boundaries), and verse choice restricted to earthly meanings that match the scene (patience, loss, bread, water, orphans, parents), never Paradise/Hell imagery over worldly scenes. Have every cut point checked against waqf rules by a hafiz. Under the strict islamqa guideline, keep female silhouettes off-screen during the recitation.
- Never use Quran as a looping bed or as 'the music'; use nasheed (vocals-only) for the bed. islamweb explicitly exempts nasheeds from the end-of-verse stopping rule, so nasheeds can be ducked, faded and interrupted by gameplay freely.
- Hadith on screen: show the Arabic and translation with source and number (e.g. Sahih al-Bukhari/Muslim), only authentic (sahih) narrations, and have a qualified person check each one; no purity rules apply to screen text (islamqa 106961).
- Voices and AI: ElevenLabs (or any TTS) is acceptable for character dialogue per the Darul Fiqh ruling; do not clone any reciter, scholar or singer (Israr Ahmed, Alafasy, etc.) without written permission (islamqa 512399), do not apply auto-tune or pitch effects to any Quran audio (islamqa 391117), and do not generate Quran with AI at all for the shipped game, since institutional fatwas (MUI 2023; Dar al-Ifta Jan 2026 as reported) permit synthetic recitation only as a reviewed learning tool. Use human recordings under an explicit licence; famous lecture clips (Israr Ahmed) need the rights-holder's permission and were not researched here.
- Google's generative music tool is not a fit for a halal score unless prompted to produce voice-only output and then audited by ear for instrument imitation; a friend singing plus layered self-harmonies recorded in a quiet room is both cheaper and safer, and matches how Awakening's vocals-only versions and artists like Ahmed Bukhatir work.
- Female singing: rewrite Teta's two songs so the sung melody is carried by a child (Layla), by a male voice, or by Teta speaking the lyric as poetry over a vocal drone; treat the women's henna-night singing as diegetic sound heard through a wall or door from the men's side, with zaghareet and duff in the clear. This removes the single biggest objection under both strict and permissive views.
- Day 9/10 wedding structure that is authentic and halal: Day 9 night = henna night indoors (women only: Teta, Rania, Um Samir, Layla; thobe, henna on hands, duff, henna songs, zaghareet) in parallel with the men's sahra outdoors (sahja call-and-response led by a zajal, no instruments); Day 10 morning = groom's public shaving joke beat, zaffa procession through the courtyard with the groom carried on shoulders, men's dabke line to Dal'ona/Zareef al-Tool sung by lead and chorus with stamping; bride and women celebrate inside the school building, the bride is shown only from the women's side (Layla's POV), the couple never touch on screen, and the wedding ends with dua and food. Do not label Tala al-Badru as a Gazan wedding custom; characters may still sing it as a beloved nasheed.
- Layla's design: at 12 she is at the age where Gazan school custom puts girls in a white headscarf and abaya/jilbab, so give her silhouette a headscarf outline outdoors and at school, and hair or a ponytail only at home among family and women; adult women (Teta, Rania, Um Samir) always wear hijab and thobe/abaya outside the home. This also solves the 'ponytail as identifying trait' problem by making the kite string and the kite itself her readable trait.
- Content lines a devout audience will check: no romance or touching between the bride and groom on screen, no alcohol anywhere (not even as background props), no mixed dancing, no women's dancing shown (heard only), no instruments in any diegetic scene including Karim 'fixing the speaker' (make the speaker play a nasheed or the news, not a song with instruments), and no Quran under gameplay. Put these in the design pillars and in the Steam page's content note so the halal claim is verifiable.
