# licensing

## Summary
Audio licensing and tooling truth for SUMUD, researched 2026-09-29 by opening primary pages wherever possible. Headline results: (1) No famous reciter (Alafasy, Abdul Basit, Sudais, Al-Dosari, Al-Ghamdi) publishes an open or commercial-use licence; quran.com, quranicaudio.com and everyayah.com all restrict to personal/non-commercial use or link-back-only with an unknown underlying recitation licence, and Alafasy's own company (Alafasy Berhad, Malaysia) reserves all rights. The only "free" Quran audio found is a small set of lesser-known reciters (Copyright Free Quran) with a credit-and-link policy written for YouTube, not games. (2) Dr Israr Ahmed's material is "All Rights Reserved" by Tanzeem-e-Islami with no published reuse policy; permission must be requested at markaz@tanzeem.org, and his official YouTube channel was terminated in April 2022 for hate-speech policy violations, which is a platform and press risk for a Steam game. (3) ElevenLabs: any paid plan (from $6 Starter) carries a commercial licence for speech; the free plan is non-commercial and requires attribution; Professional Voice Cloning is strictly your own voice ("Even with their consent, you cannot clone someone else's voice"); Arabic is supported but with no dialect control, so Gazan dialect is not a feature; Eleven Music's self-serve commercial licence explicitly excludes "film, TV, radio, & Studio Games" (defined as games commercialised across multiple platforms), so in-game use needs written clarification or an Enterprise deal. (4) "Google Flow" is a video/image studio (Veo, Imagen); the music product is Google Flow Music (Lyria 3/3 Pro/3.5), with commercial-use rights granted through Google AI Pro/Ultra memberships and Google disclaiming ownership; MusicFX was shut down 31 July 2026. Suno Pro/Premier grants commercial rights and can export vocal-only stems; Udio has become a walled garden with no export and is unusable for a game. Nothing reliably constrains a generator to vocals-only at generation time; stem separation or "a cappella" prompting is the workaround. (5) Steam requires disclosure of any AI-made content that ships in the game (art, sound, narrative, localisation) via the Content Survey; documented backlash in 2025-2026 includes The Alters (11 bit, the This War of Mine studio), Jurassic World Evolution 3, Arc Raiders (AI voices replaced by actors), Party Animals (review-bombed, contest cancelled) and Vapor World (AI cutscenes removed). (6) Scholars: IslamQA permits AI Quran recitation only with the voice owner's explicit permission and a lawful purpose; Al-Azhar condemned AI-made "Quranic songs"; cloning any person's voice without permission is ruled haram; Quran must not be played as background under distraction or alongside music, and imagery must match the verses. No fatwa specifically on AI nasheed was found, but the nasheed conditions (no instruments, no vocal effects imitating instruments) apply. (7) Clean CC0 ambience exists: a 12-sound "Gaza" pack recorded in Gaza Strip in 2016 (Freesound, CC0), a Palestinian recordist's Ramallah/Tulkarem set (CC0), Sonniss GDC bundles, Kenney and Pixabay; Ambient Gaza has no licence published and must be asked; BBC, earth.fm and Cities & Memory are non-commercial or restricted. Recommendation: prototype with CC0 ambience, on-screen Quran text only, and human-sung placeholders; ship with human voices, a commissioned or explicitly licensed recitation, written permission for any scholar clip, and zero AI-generated audio.

- [likely] Abdul Basit Abdus Samad's Egyptian-radio-era recordings may have expired producer rights in Egypt (50 years), but this is not a safe basis for shipping them worldwide.
  Egyptian Law 82/2002 Art. 167: "Producers of sound recordings enjoy an exclusive economic right for 50 years from the date on which the recording was made or made public" (Wikimedia Commons summary). He died in 1988; recordings before 1976 would be out of producer protection in Egypt. However: remastered re-issues carry fresh claims, the term differs by country of sale (India and the US use different rules; not verified here), moral rights persist, and Content ID claims exist regardless. Do not rely on a public-domain theory without a lawyer.
  src: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Copyright_rules_by_territory/Egypt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Basit_'Abd_us-Samad

- [verified] IslamQA rules that AI-generated Quran recitation in a human voice is permissible only with the voice owner's explicit permission, for a lawful purpose and without harm; the voice owner earns no recitation reward but shares in the reward for benefit.
  IslamQA 512399: "it is not permissible to make voices, or to manipulate them or alter them, except with the permission of their owners"; with permission and a lawful purpose there is "nothing wrong with that"; the person "will not attain a reward for reciting Quran" but is rewarded for benefitting people; giving permission for haram use makes the owner "a partner in that sin". IslamQA 391117: auto-tune on Quran is not permissible because it alters letters and tajwid; any effect that repeats letter sounds is haram. - [verified] AI voiceovers for ordinary content are treated as permissible automated reproduction by some muftis, and no fatwa specifically on AI-generated nasheed vocals was found; the existing nasheed conditions (no instruments, no vocal effects imitating instruments, modest presentation) would govern it.
  Darul Fiqh: AI voiceovers are permissible for educational videos because they are "a form of automated reproduction rather than genuine speech" (parrot-recitation analogy). - [verified] Quran inside a game must be a stop-and-listen moment: scholars object to Quran played as background during play or distraction, alongside music, or with mismatched imagery.
  IslamQA 102874: playing Quran where people are busy or where music plays is "a kind of disrespect", citing Hanbal on reciting in noisy marketplaces. Islamweb 86274: attentive listening is obligatory in the Hanafi view; hearing Quran while playing is discouraged though better than music. IslamQA 175174: recitation in video clips is fine when images match the verses' meaning; no music, no depiction of women, no worldly imagery standing in for the unseen. Design consequence: pause gameplay, mute rumble/SFX, show the verse text, match the image to the verse, and do not place a verse under a strike sequence or any daf/nasheed cue; whether silhouetted female characters may be on screen during the verse is a point to put to the sensitivity readers.

## Recommendations
- PROTOTYPE THIS WEEK (never leaves the machine): ambience from the Freesound CC0 'Gaza' pack (kyles, packs/22953) and sounds_from_palestine, wind/sea/rumble synthesised in code, impacts and debris from Sonniss GDC bundles or Kenney; Quran shown as on-screen Arabic text with a full hush (no recitation audio yet); Teta's songs as a friend's phone-recorded hum or a temporary Eleven Music a cappella on the $22 Creator plan tagged PLACEHOLDER and excluded from git; no ElevenLabs speech at all since the design ships subtitles only.
- SHIPPED GAME, QURAN: commission a living reciter to record the exact ayat the game needs under a written licence (ideal: a Palestinian or Gazan qari, which is also the honest, marketable choice), or approach Alafasy Berhad (+603 8682 3105) or a CFQ reciter for written game-use permission naming Steam, mobile and trailers. Do not ship quran.com, quranicaudio, everyayah, mp3quran or Quran Central files; do not rely on an Abdul Basit public-domain theory; never generate or clone a recitation.
- SHIPPED GAME, DR ISRAR AHMED AND OTHER SCHOLARS: use a real clip only with written permission from Tanzeem-e-Islami (markaz@tanzeem.org) and decide with eyes open about the April 2022 YouTube termination and how hostile press will frame it; a safer 'reference' is a short attributed quotation from his published tafsir shown as text on a card, with the same text shown as a hadith or verse card would be. Never synthesise any scholar's voice.
- SHIPPED GAME, SONGS AND VOICES: human voices only. The friend sings Teta's lullaby and the wedding song a cappella (duff permitted for the women's wedding scene per the cited rulings); record on a decent USB mic in a quiet room and clean it in code. If ElevenLabs is ever used for a voice, the speaker must create and verify their own Professional Voice Clone and share it, and the game must carry a Steam AI disclosure. Given Arc Raiders replaced AI lines with actors and The Alters was punished for undisclosed AI text, a Gaza game with AI voices is the worst possible headline.
- GENERATIVE MUSIC: if any AI music is used even as a temp track, treat it as disposable. Eleven Music's self-serve licence excludes 'Studio Games' and needs written clarification for a multi-platform release; Google Flow Music needs a paid Google AI membership for commercial rights and Google Flow itself is a video tool; Suno Pro/Premier works and can export vocal-only stems but faces 2026 download caps and is watermark/claim-prone; Udio cannot export at all. None can be hard-constrained to vocals-only; use 'a cappella' prompting plus stem separation if you must. Recommendation: do not ship any AI-generated audio.
- QURAN IN-GAME RULES (put in the GDD): a verse or hadith appears only in a paused, silent 'listen' state with rumble, SFX and any vocal cue muted; imagery on screen must match the verse; no verse under a strike, a wedding or a duff cue; verses and hadith carry a printed reference (surah:ayah, collection and number); ask the sensitivity readers whether silhouetted female characters may be on screen during recitation, since one cited ruling forbids depicting women in Quran clips.
- STEAM DISCLOSURE: keep a written asset ledger (file, source, licence, date, who approved) from day one; if zero AI content ships, answer the Content Survey 'no' with a clean conscience and say so on the store page, because a truthful 'no generative AI' statement is a selling point for this audience; if any AI asset survives, disclose it precisely (category, scope, review) rather than vaguely.
- AMBIENCE PROVENANCE: on Freesound use only recordings whose description shows the uploader recorded them (location, date, gear); reject anything 'screen captured' or ripped from streams, and reject real grief or casualty clips regardless of licence; write to Ambient Gaza only if you want authentic Gaza beds and are prepared to be refused; credit every CC0 recordist anyway in the credits and the sources tab.
- LEGAL CHECK BEFORE LAUNCH: one hour with an Indian IP lawyer on (a) the reciter licence wording, (b) whether the charity revenue share needs a legal entity, and (c) the copyright term for any pre-1976 recording you are tempted by, since terms differ in India, the US and the EU and this research did not verify them.
