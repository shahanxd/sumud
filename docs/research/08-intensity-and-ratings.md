# research_v2_3be70ed9f06

## Summary
A. Timed inputs survive in calm narrative games when four things are true: the moment is taught before it is tested (Inside's forgiving checkpoints work; A Plague Tale's un-signposted instant-death chases and Inside's "run or hide?" ambiguity are what reviewers hate), failure fails forward or costs seconds rather than a life (Until Dawn pushes you onto a harder path, Life is Strange's rooftop lets Kate die and continues, Detroit's Casual mode exists precisely so QTEs cannot kill), the rhythm belongs to the fiction (Valiant Hearts' Can-Can taxi chases were called "as taxing (and enjoyable) as a 1980s LCD portable" and "tough to take... seriously"), and timing never gates reading (Oxenfree's timed dialogue is the one system non-native readers complain about, which matters for a bilingual Arabic/English game). Florence proves a mechanic can carry intensity with no fail state at all. The accessibility bodies converge on one option set: hold-or-press toggle, extended or paused timers, auto-complete/skip, no button mashing, no simultaneous or motion inputs, large centred prompts; the 2024 Until Dawn remake ships all of these and is the template. Steam's tags "Playable without Quick Time Events" and "Playable at Your Own Pace" can only be claimed honestly if every timed moment can be disabled. I propose ten day-mapped moments (kite duel, first strike, candle carry, jerrycan race, drone hush, the sea limit, the walk to the school, generator cord, dabke clap, final flight) each with mechanic, rationale, failure handling and fallback, plus the standard Godot 4 build (AnimationPlayer property/call-method/audio/animation tracks, a dedicated disabled Camera2D switched with make_current, ColorRect letterbox bars animated by AnimationPlayer, hold-or-press skip).

B. Neither PEGI nor ESRB has a "child death" descriptor. PEGI's operative trigger is "violence towards defenceless characters" (18), and every opened rationale confirms that what is rated is the on-screen act, its realism and its detail, not the theme or the art style: Inside's stylised boy being sliced and mauled is PEGI 18 / ESRB M, Detroit's abusive-father scene is PEGI 18, Life is Strange 2's child shot with visible blood is PEGI 18, This War of Mine (and The Little Ones) is PEGI 18 for "violence against innocent human characters", while Valiant Hearts, which executes its bound protagonist by firing squad on screen and has timed-press amputations, is PEGI 12 / ESRB T because "none of the violence is realistic", and Life is Strange, which shows a teenager's suicide jump and a character shot, is PEGI 16. Liyla, the closest precedent on this exact subject, kills the child off-screen in an ambulance hit by a missile; This War of Mine took the opposite path (children cannot die, adults carry them away), which players read as a lack of consequence. Recommendation: make the doc's one off-screen death a child's (Sami, Day 7), show the before and the after in silhouette (a small wrapped shape carried at a distance, the janazah, the kite on the wall, the name), imply the how through sound, light, dust and adults' bodies blocking Layla's view, and never make the death preventable by a timed input. That keeps SUMUD at PEGI 16 or below and ESRB T; a visible impact on the child's body, blood or injury detail, a soldier or parent shown deliberately harming a child, gore, torture, lingering on pain, or a failable "save the child" QTE are the things that push it to 18 / M.

## Findings
- [verified] Game Accessibility Guidelines: 'Do not make precise timing essential to gameplay – offer alternatives, actions that can be carried out while paused, or a skip mechanism' (Advanced, Motor).
  Rationale is to avoid timing requirements where avoidable and make optional timed sections optional; 'Making them optional can even be possible in what seem to be extremely time dependent games.' Cited best practice: Deadly Scramble (no-timer mode), Final Fantasy XV (wait mode), Dragon Age 2, Fallout 3 (VATS).
  src: https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/do-not-make-precise-timing-essential-to-gameplay-offer-alternatives-actions-that-can-be-carried-out-while-paused-or-a-skip-mechanism/

- [verified] Game Accessibility Guidelines: 'Avoid repeated inputs (button-mashing/quick time events)' (Intermediate, Motor); 'If you can't avoid QTEs or button mashing actions, include a setting to simplify or skip/disable them.'
  Rationale: rapid precision 'for gamers with a wide range of strength, fatigue, pain, speed or accuracy related impairments can be a huge barrier or even exclude completely, especially in the case of quick time events'. Examples: Castlevania Lords of Shadow 2 QTE toggle, The Witcher 2 QTE difficulty, Uncharted 4 accessibility settings. Player testimony includes an MS patient unable to play with rapid keypresses.
  src: https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-repeated-inputs-button-mashing-quick-time-events/

- [verified] Game Accessibility Guidelines also ask for a skip for non-core elements and a hold/toggle/auto alternative for held buttons.
  'Offer a means to bypass gameplay elements that aren't part of the core mechanic, via settings or in-game skip option' (Intermediate) cites LA Noire's skip-action-sequence option, Puzzle Retreat (unlimited time, reset, skip), Deadly Scramble, Mass Effect Andromeda, Nier: Automata. 'Avoid / provide alternatives to requiring buttons to be held down' (Intermediate, Motor): 'Holding requires much greater motor ability than pressing'; offer hold, toggle and automatic modes; examples The Long Dark accessible interactions, Uncharted 4, Battlefield 4, CS:GO, Titanfall.
  src: https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/offer-a-means-to-bypass-gameplay-elements-that-arent-part-of-the-core-mechanic-via-settings-or-in-game-skip-option/; https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/avoid-provide-alternatives-to-requiring-buttons-to-be-held-down/

- [verified] Xbox Accessibility Guideline 107 (Input) tells developers to avoid QTE-style rapid presses, long holds and simultaneous presses, and if unavoidable to provide a less demanding alternative or a bypass.
  Verbatim: 'Avoid introducing mechanics where a player is required to repeat or execute multiple keystrokes or button presses within a short period of time (like quick-time events (QTEs)).' 'If inputs that require repeated button strokes, prolonged button presses, or simultaneous button presses cannot be avoided, provide an alternative input option that is either less physically demanding or allow the player to bypass game events that require the use of using these mechanics, or that part of the game experience altogether.' Note: 'Quick taps, as well as prolonged button holds, can be difficult for players with mobility-related disabilities. Additional options that don't rely on either of these mechanisms or allow the player to skip "quick tap challenge" areas will provide an even more accessible experience.' Examples: Gears 5 'Button Tap Challenges' setting (press & hold vs quick taps); The Long Dark 'accessible interactions' converting all press-and-hold actions into presses; also recommends letting players control game speed, remap actions not just buttons, and toggles/auto-holds. Mobile section: avoid or provide alternatives for prolonged holds, rapid taps, multi-finger gestures and gyroscopic tilt/shake.
  src: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107

- [verified] Xbox Accessibility Guideline 116 (Time limits) applies to non-core UI timers and sets concrete numbers; it explicitly exempts core-mechanic timers but its examples show the cutscene/dialogue pattern to copy.
  For UI time limits: warn in multiple ways; let the player request a longer or no limit; adjust up to at least 10x the default; be warned and given at least 20 seconds to extend with a simple action, extendable at least ten times; or turn it off. Dialogue/tutorial display durations should be adjustable to 10x or dismissable on input (Ori and the Will of the Wisps: NPC dialogue has no time limit). Dragon Quest XI S is shown with 'Hold to skip' on cutscenes and an 'Autoplay cutscenes' toggle. The guideline states 'an actual gameplay mechanic (for example, a countdown timer of three minutes to finish a track in a racing game) isn't applicable'.
  src: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/116

- [verified] Steam's accessibility feature tags define 'Playable without Quick Time Events' and 'Playable at Your Own Pace'; SUMUD can only claim them if every timed moment can be disabled.
  Steamworks doc: 'Quick time events, which require a sequence of precisely-timed button presses or button mashing, can be uncomfortable or impossible for players.' and 'Playable at Your Own Pace: Some players are not able to quickly perform inputs, or may be uncomfortable with many precise inputs. Players can look for this tag to know that they can take as long as they need for each button press.' Access-Ability (Nov 2025) reports Valve's developer guidance as 'Answer yes if your game doesn't feature Quick Time Events, or if they can be disabled' and documents misuse by games with parry windows and timed dialogue (Silksong, Dispatch); the author asks Valve to clarify whether single time-sensitive inputs count.
  src: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/accessibility_features; https://access-ability.uk/2025/11/07/steam-devs-are-misusing-the-playable-without-timed-inputs-accessibility-tag-and-it-might-be-valves-fault/

- [verified] The Until Dawn 2024 remake is the current reference option set for QTEs and 'Don't Move' segments.
  Access-Ability's review lists: QTE timer can pause on timeout for infinite time; auto-succeed on timeout; auto-succeed immediately without the timer; auto-fail or randomise; every QTE can be set to one player-chosen 'QTE prompt button'; prompts can be made dramatically larger and always centred; Don't Move segments can auto-complete or be replaced by nudging an on-screen capsule with the left stick. The original game's Don't Move relied on controller gyro: Steam players report keyboard/mouse and Xbox-pad users cannot complete it and Steam Deck users must hold sticks still; workarounds cited are the 'auto succeed' accessibility option and 'passive' controller mode.
  src: https://access-ability.uk/2024/10/11/until-dawn-ps5-accessibility-review/; https://steamcommunity.com/app/2172010/discussions/0/4851030529135895077/

- [verified] Until Dawn's QTEs work because failure fails forward and is telegraphed; they break when a death has no connection to input.
  Wikipedia: 'Deaths are permanent; the game's narrative will adapt to these changes and continue forward without them'; failed prompts do not automatically kill but put characters on 'harder, more treacherous paths'; Game Informer called Don't Move 'one of the best in gaming', GameSpot praised its tension, IGN criticised 'poorly-implemented motion control' and QTEs 'tedious at times'. Gamecritics' long analysis faults Matt's death, which 'has absolutely no clear connection to the choices anyone makes' and offers 'no QTE no chance to escape', as the one place the system feels arbitrary. Game Developer's 'Fixing QTE' argues reaction QTEs make 'all you care about... the controller button that pops up' rather than the scene, and that choice-based QTEs where 'the point of it is choice, the timer is just there to add pressure' and prompts placed spatially where the action is are the fix.
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Until_Dawn; https://gamecritics.com/daniel-weissenberger/until-dawn-the-definitive-article/; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/fixing-qte

- [verified] Detroit: Become Human ships a Casual mode that removes QTE lethality; critics' main complaints were motion-controlled QTEs and their sheer abundance.
  Casual 'makes all Quick Time Events (QTE) much simpler with more time', 'you will only use the X command or the O command in most cases', with 'fewer chances for you to kill off one of your characters'; Experienced has 'less time and more controls' including motion control and 'you can kill off a character easier'. Wikipedia reception: EGM's Goroff disparaged the motion-sensitive feature 'especially... its use in quick-time events', Game Informer found motion controls 'unintuitive', Tamburro criticised their 'abundance'; the flowchart lets players 'rewind to certain points in the story to reshape decisions in the event of regret'.
  src: https://www.gamersdecide.com/articles/detroit-become-human-difficulty; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit:_Become_Human

- [verified] Inside's chases are tolerated because respawns are instant and checkpoints generous, and hated exactly where the game does not say whether to run or hide.
  Cubed3: 'Respawns are quick and checkpoints are generous, so it rarely becomes annoying'; deaths that look like trial-and-error 'upon closer inspection the reason for failure is always attributed to user error'; but some escape scenes are 'nonsensical' because the 2.5D view implies routes that do not exist. Gamecritics counted dying 'literally forty times in a 3.5-hour game', and on the masked-men chases: 'the game doesn't always make it clear if it wants players to run, hide, or perform some kind of contextual escape'; Playdead 'intentionally blurs that line so that the player can learn from it'. Wikipedia: 'If the character dies, the game continues from the most recent checkpoint'; deaths 'are presented realistically and are often graphic', which earned an ESRB Mature rating 'as opposed to Limbo's Teen rating'.
  src: https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/nintendo-switch/inside; https://gamecritics.com/corey-motley/inside-review/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game)

- [verified] Valiant Hearts' rhythm driving sections are the canonical example of a timed section that clashes with a serious war story; its timed healing minigame drew a similar complaint.
  Kotaku: the driving is 'about as taxing (and enjoyable) as a 1980s LCD portable, even with the novelty of enemy attacks coming in time to frantic classical music', and a puzzle played while 'a child screaming in the distance, seemingly on the brink of death' undercuts the tragedy. TheSixthAxis liked the Paris taxi ride where obstacles appear 'in time with the music and then... it segues in to waltz', but found it 'rather disturbing to hear the soldiers cry as you remove bullets and perform amputations by pressing square, circle, triangle'. Wikipedia confirms 'rhythmic car chase sections set to classic songs' and that Anna's healing 'requires the player to time button presses'.
  src: https://kotaku.com/valiant-hearts-the-kotaku-review-1595774231; http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/48930/valiant-hearts-the-great-war-switch-review; https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2014/06/25/valiant-hearts-the-great-war-review/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valiant_Hearts:_The_Great_War

- [verified] Life is Strange has no conventional QTEs; its most intense scene (Kate on the roof) works by removing the safety net once, and the game continues after failure.
  Steam players: 'there is sometimes u need to hurry up with things but not in a sense that u cant rewind time'; a 5-minute idle timeout returns to the menu without picking a choice. Kotaku: on the roof Max can only freeze time, 'How much you remember about what you saw in that room is critical to what happens next', a wrong answer means 'Kate commits suicide, and there's no going back', and the author kept playing: 'Kate Marsh is dead. I have to live with it.' CBR: the jump is seen from Max's viewpoint ('seeing Kate at the top of the roof, suddenly plummeting to the ground'), a memorial follows, and end-of-episode stats tell the player they could have saved her. Wikipedia: the developers consulted experts 'to tackle the subject of teen suicide'.
  src: https://steamcommunity.com/app/319630/discussions/0/282992646984622255/; https://kotaku.com/life-is-strange-can-go-way-way-wrong-1696574304; https://www.cbr.com/life-is-strange-fail-save-kate/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Strange_(video_game)

- [verified] Oxenfree's timed dialogue exists to allow interruption and silence; its one persistent complaint is from players who cannot read the options in time, especially non-native English readers.
  Sean Krankel (MCV): 'we wanted not saying anything at all to be a valid choice for the player as well'; interrupts required 'a line for almost any moment'. Wikipedia: 'we wanted to never have a cut scene in the game'; critics praised naturalism but found consequences opaque. Steam thread: 'Sometimes I just selected the first option because there was no time to read the other options'; 'I'd like to be able to consider something before I choose an answer. It's not good design'; the workaround is that pausing shows the semi-transparent options.
  src: https://mcvuk.com/business-news/i-thought-we-were-going-to-go-out-of-business-sean-krankel-on-the-development-of-oxenfree/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxenfree; https://steamcommunity.com/app/388880/discussions/0/142261027575720514/

- [verified] Florence shows intensity can come from mechanics-as-metaphor with no fail state or reflex demand.
  Ken Wong: 'A lot of the small mechanics are metaphors for what's going on emotionally for the players'; 'None of the mechanics [in Florence] are what you would call "deep"'; 'Use whatever mechanics feel right for the story'; 'Revisiting mechanics were also a great way to show characters going through change'. The speech-bubble jigsaw 'feels like putting words together, but... as the dates go on... the puzzles become easier and easier'; the argument uses increasingly sharp bubble shapes. Wong's aim: games 'anyone can play' without needing to 'practice it for 100 hours'.
  src: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-simple-mechanics-and-vignettes-inspired-i-florence-i-; https://milanote.com/the-work/lead-designer-of-monument-valley-deconstructs-his-latest-game-florence

- [verified] Godot 4 in-engine cinematics are built from AnimationPlayer tracks, a dedicated Camera2D switched with make_current/enabled, and ColorRect letterbox bars animated by the same AnimationPlayer.
  Official docs: AnimationPlayer track types are Property, Position/Rotation/Scale 3D, Blend Shape, Call Method ('call a function at a precise time from within an animation'), Bezier, Audio Playback (via AudioStreamPlayer) and Animation Playback ('sequence the animations of other animation player nodes'); play(), queue() ('once the current animation and all previously queued animations are done'), animation_finished signal, speed_scale (0 pauses, negative reverses); note that stop() does not process method/audio/animation tracks. Camera2D: 'Only one camera can be active per viewport', activate via enabled or make_current(); position_smoothing_enabled for eased moves; limit_* and limit_smoothed; zoom; offset 'useful for looking around or camera shake animations'; drag margins for dead zones. Godot forum: letterbox = ColorRects/TextureRects as black bars moved by an AnimationPlayer 'fade' animation played forward at cutscene start and in reverse at the end; cutscene pattern = AnimationPlayer + a disabled 'CutsceneCam', disable player controls during playback, keep dialogue in a separate system and optionally let an input advance lines.
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/animation_track_types.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_animationplayer.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_camera2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/introduction.html

- [verified] PEGI's age labels: 12 = non-realistic violence towards human-like characters; 16 = violence that 'looks the same as would be expected in real life'; 18 = 'gross violence, apparently motiveless killing, or violence towards defenceless characters'.   pegi.info verbatim: PEGI 12 'non-realistic violence towards human-like characters'; PEGI 16 'applied once the depiction of violence (or sexual activity) reaches a stage that looks the same as would be expected in real life'; PEGI 18 'gross violence, apparently motiveless killing, or violence towards defenceless characters'; Violence descriptor: 'games rated PEGI 16 or 18 have increasingly more realistic-looking violence'. ## Recommendations
- DESIGN RULES FOR INTENSITY IN SUMUD (derived from the findings): (1) Teach before you test: every timed moment is rehearsed once with no stakes earlier in the same day (Inside's failures are forgiven because respawn is instant; A Plague Tale and Inside are hated exactly where the player cannot tell whether to run or hide). (2) Failure costs seconds, never a life and never a person: the doc's white-dust checkpoint rule stands; no timed input may ever decide whether a character lives (Until Dawn's one arbitrary death and Detroit's failable child scene are the two things reviewers and child-protection bodies punished). (3) Rhythm must be diegetic: hands, feet, the sea, the oven, the generator cord, clapping, never a music-driven obstacle course (Valiant Hearts' Can-Can chases); this also sidesteps the founder's no-instruments constraint. (4) Timing never gates reading: no timed dialogue choices in a bilingual Arabic/English game (Oxenfree's non-native-reader complaints); choices pause. (5) One input per moment: press or hold, never mash, never two buttons, never gyro (XAG 107; Until Dawn's gyro Don't Move excluded keyboard and Xbox-pad players). (6) Intensity budget: at most one timed beat per day, none on Day 7 night or Day 10, so surprise stays surprising.
- GLOBAL OPTION SET (ship at launch, resolves the doc's 'no timed button sequences' promise against the founder's request): a single 'Own pace' toggle that, for every timed moment, pauses the timer until input (Until Dawn remake's pause-on-timeout), plus per-moment auto-complete; a 'Press instead of hold' toggle (The Long Dark pattern); a timing-window multiplier from 1x to at least 3x (XAG 116's 10x spirit for UI); prompts that are large, centred and use the doc's eight remappable inputs only; hold-or-press 'skip cinematic' (Dragon Quest XI S 'Hold to skip' with a press alternative per GAG); subtitles/haptic cues for every audio telegraph (birds, rumble, drone hum, gunboat loudspeaker) for deaf players; the existing screen-shake and flash toggles. With 'Own pace' available, SUMUD can honestly tick Steam's 'Playable without Quick Time Events' and 'Playable at Your Own Pace' features; without it, it cannot.
- MOMENT 1, Day 1, 'Glass string' (kite duel with Sami on the beach). Mechanic: the two kites circle in the wind field; when the strings cross, a one-button tug inside a wide window (about 0.8 s at 1x) cuts Sami's line; holding instead pays out string to escape his cut. Why here: it is the game's first timed input, in the joy beat, with pride as the only stake, and it teaches the tug/pay-out verbs the kite uses for the rest of the game; kite fighting is a real pastime the sources tab can cite. Failure: Layla's kite is cut, Sami crows, the kite drifts to the rocks and Layla retrieves it (a 20-second walk), and the duel can be replayed at once; the notebook records the loss as a joke, not a defeat. Fallback: 'Own pace' pauses on the crossing until input; 'Press instead of hold' for pay-out; auto-complete lets the duel play out as a cinematic win or loss the player chooses.
- MOMENT 2, Day 3, 'Birds leave the roof' (the first strike, before the title card). Mechanic: on the rooftop at dusk the birds lift, the rumble bus rises, the letterbox bars slide in, and the player has roughly 12 seconds of ordinary movement to get Layla down the stairwell to the family; the strike lands on the far end of the street when she arrives or when the timer ends. Why here: it is the tonal turn the whole game hinges on, it uses only 'move' (no new input), it has been rehearsed by two earlier rooftop descents that day, and it is over before the player can fail to understand it. Failure: white dust, checkpoint on the roof a few seconds back, birds lift again; no death animation. Fallback: 'Own pace' converts the timer into a trigger (the strike lands only after Layla reaches the stairwell), auto-complete plays it as a cutscene of Baba pulling her in; photosensitivity mode removes the flash; rumble bus can be lowered separately.
- MOMENT 3, Day 3 night, 'Shield the flame' (candle traversal into the collapsed bakery). Mechanic: Layla carries a candle through drafts shown by dust and plastic; when a draft gust is telegraphed (a sound and a lean in the flame), the player holds one button to cup the flame, releasing to move on; darkness is impassable, per the doc. Why here: it turns the doc's light-as-resource rule into a physical act of care, it is tense without any threat to the body, and it is the first hold input, introduced after the first press input. Failure: the flame dies, the screen goes to the doc's indigo, Layla relights from the last candle stub she passed (a few metres back); the only cost is wax, which the street notices ('you used two candles?'). Fallback: 'Press instead of hold' cups the flame with a tap; 'Own pace' makes drafts wait for the input; auto-shield makes the candle immune.
- MOMENT 4, Day 4, 'Empty jerrycans' (the kids' race back from the water truck). Mechanic: a 30-second foot race against Sami and three named kids over rubble and planks, with timed jumps whose cue is the rattle of the cans; a good landing keeps speed, a bad one costs a stumble. Why here: the day is about carrying heavy things slowly, so a race carrying nothing is the release valve and the joy beat the doc already names; it uses the platforming the player has practised for three days. Failure: Layla loses and the kids tease her; nothing else, and the notebook remembers who won (it changes a line at the wedding). Fallback: 'Own pace' turns jumps into auto-jump over the marked gaps; auto-complete chooses a random winner; the race can be skipped by walking.
- MOMENT 5, Day 5, 'The hum' (delivering a message by kite while a drone passes). Mechanic: an Until Dawn 'Don't Move' inversion built without gyro: when the hum rises, Layla must release every input and let the kite hang in the wind, holding still on the roof until the sound passes; any input during the pass yanks the kite into view. Why here: the enemy has no face but now has a sound, which honours the founder's 'not completely faceless' note without a character; stillness is the most Sumud-like verb there is; and it is surprise through subtraction, the opposite of every other input in the game. Failure: the kite is spotted and the message drops into the street; Layla or Karim retrieves it from a neighbour's roof, a two-minute detour; the kite is never destroyed here, because the doc says losing the kite must matter and this is not the place. Fallback: subtitle and controller-vibration cue for the hum; 'Own pace' makes the pass wait until the player has let go; auto-complete plays the pass as a held camera shot.
- MOMENT 6, Day 6, 'Three miles' (Baba hauls the net, then turns back before the spotlight). Mechanic: part one, a swell rhythm: hold to haul on the crest, release in the trough, cued by the boat's pitch and the water sound, five to eight pulls; part two, a buoy line marks the limit, a loudspeaker crackles and a spotlight sweeps from off-screen right, and Baba has one generous window to swing the tiller and turn home. Why here: the doc wants the sea as both joy and border; the gunboat as light, sound and voice is the indirect showing of soldiers the founder asked for; and it lets Baba 'go straight in' and confront the limit without combat. Failure: the net tears and half the catch is lost (fewer fish at the shared table, remembered by the street), or the boat is soaked by the water cannon and drifts back inside the line with the catch spoiled; checkpoint at the last pull; Baba is never shot. Fallback: 'Press instead of hold' for hauling; 'Own pace' pauses the spotlight until the turn input; auto-complete hauls and turns for the player.
- MOMENT 7, Day 7, 'What you can carry' (the walk to the UN school) and the night that follows. Mechanic: no timed input; the intensity is weight. The player has chosen one neighbour and one bag with their hands; the walk is a long single-direction hold with the camera pulled back into letterbox, a second strike far off, and the choice-made-with-hands rule from the doc; at the school gate the load is set down and the player is not allowed to go back. Why here: after six days of small timed moments the absence of one is the surprise; Florence and the Kate rooftop show that removing the tool is what makes a scene land. Failure: none possible. Fallback: hold can be a toggle (auto-walk); the walk can be skipped as a cinematic; this is also the night the child's death (Part B) is discovered, and that discovery has no input at all.
- MOMENT 8, Day 8, 'The cord' (Karim and Layla restart the generator). Mechanic: Karim counts in Arabic, the player pulls the starter cord on his count (hold to draw, release to pull), three to five pulls, then a torch-lit wiring puzzle with no timer; on success the speaker crackles and the courtyard lights come up in a cinematic that pans across every lit window. Why here: it is the day light returns, the rhythm is a human voice and a cord, not music, and the payoff (lights and sound) is the biggest cinematic the game can afford in silhouette. Failure: the cord snaps back, Karim laughs, try again immediately; no fuel is lost. Fallback: 'Press instead of hold'; 'Own pace' makes the count wait; auto-complete starts the generator on the first pull.
- MOMENT 9, Day 9, 'Dabke practice' (the courtyard, the night before the wedding). Mechanic: a clapping and stamping rhythm section: the beat is hands and feet only, the prompt is Teta's cane tapping, the player stamps on the tap and holds on the long step; missing beats is visible as Layla falling out of line. Why here: it is the doc's joy beat and the one place a rhythm section is tonally right, because the rhythm belongs to the people in the scene (the opposite of the Valiant Hearts Can-Can), and it uses hands, feet and a cane, so it needs no instrument. Failure: laughter, Teta's correction, the line restarts; the number of clean rounds decides only how many neighbours join the line at the wedding. Fallback: 'Own pace' lets the line wait for each stamp; auto-complete puts Layla in the line as a spectator; no rhythm at all if the player just sits, which is also a valid choice.
- MOMENT 10, Day 10, 'The last flight'. Mechanic: every kite verb at once (launch, pay out, hold on gusts, steer) in a rising wind, with the camera leaving Layla and climbing with the kite as the dedicated cinematic camera takes over and the player-designed kites rise; the credits run over the sea. Why here: it is the ending the doc already has; the only design rule to add is that the kite cannot be lost on Day 10, so the final flight has intensity (wind, height, sound) and no failure. Failure: none possible. Fallback: auto-fly with the player holding one input or none.
- GODOT 4 BUILD NOTE FOR CINEMATICS: one 'Cinematic' scene per beat containing an AnimationPlayer, a disabled Camera2D named CutsceneCam, and a CanvasLayer with two ColorRects for the letterbox bars (the ColorRect-plus-AnimationPlayer approach is the one the Godot forum recommends; putting them on a CanvasLayer so they ignore camera movement is standard practice). Property tracks move the camera, characters and lights; Call Method tracks fire game events (disable player input, set the power state, write the notebook line); Audio Playback tracks place the rumble and the recitation; Animation Playback tracks trigger character rigs' animations; play() then queue() chains beats and animation_finished hands control back. Switch cameras with make_current() (only one active per viewport), use position_smoothing for the pull-backs, offset for shake (respecting the shake toggle) and zoom for the climb on Day 10. Since every asset in the pipeline is code, author these AnimationPlayer resources as text so the nightly screenshot diff covers them. Provide 'Hold to skip' with a press alternative and, following XAG 116's Dragon Quest example, let dialogue in cinematics wait for input rather than auto-advance.
- PART B, WHO AND WHEN: make the doc's already-approved 'one off-screen death' a child's, and make it Sami. He is the most specific child in the game (rival, best friend, the other kite), which is what the founder means by 'the reality'. Place it on Day 7: the evacuation order comes, Sami's family is on the far side of the street, and by nightfall at the school it is known that they did not arrive. Do not present it as a twist (the doc's rule stands): birds, rumble and the far strike during the walk have already told the player something happened, and the game never cuts to reveal it. Day 9's 'the street rebuilds a kite' then becomes Sami's kite, and Day 10's sky carries it first.
- PART B, WHAT IS SEEN AND WHAT IS IMPLIED (silhouette rules): Seen: Sami alive and specific on Day 6 evening (the last duel or the last joke, on the roof at dusk); the strike as weather during the Day 7 walk (light, dust, rumble, birds, a pause in the column); at the school, adults' bodies closing around Layla so she, and the camera, cannot see (the child's frame from the doc); a small shape wrapped in white cloth carried across the courtyard at a distance, in full silhouette, no face, no wound, no blood; the janazah in the courtyard, where the founder's request for a Quran verse belongs; Sami's kite hung on the wall with his name; the empty spot in the Day 9 duel line and a different song from Teta. Implied, never shown: how he died, where he was found, what his body looks like, whether anyone dug. Never: the impact on a child's body, injury detail, blood, a child screaming under rubble as ambience while the player solves a puzzle (the exact Valiant Hearts moment Kotaku attacked), a soldier or any person shown deliberately harming a child, a lingering shot of the shrouded body's face, or a timed input that could have saved him. Layla's notebook writes his name; the player types nothing and chooses nothing.
- PART B, RATINGS TARGET AND WHAT MOVES IT: with the rules above, the truthful target is PEGI 16 and ESRB T (Violence, possibly no Blood descriptor at all), and PEGI 12 remains possible because the act is unseen and the depiction non-realistic (Valiant Hearts' on-screen execution is 12; Life is Strange's on-screen suicide jump is 16; Liyla's off-screen ambulance strike is the same-subject precedent). Update the doc's rating line from 'PEGI 12' to 'PEGI 12 to 16, ESRB T' so the Steam page is honest. The things that would push SUMUD to PEGI 18 / ESRB M, in order of certainty: any on-screen violence against a child with realistic injury (Life is Strange 2's shoulder wound with blood; Inside's stylised boy; both 18), a scene of a parent or soldier deliberately harming a child on screen (Detroit, 18, plus NSPCC-style condemnation), gore or dismemberment in any style (Limbo's silhouettes still reached 16 to 18), torture or a focus on pain, 'apparently motiveless killing' shown as an act, and strong language ('fuck' appears in every 18 rationale opened). A failable 'save the child' timed input would be both a rating risk and the design choice most criticised in this research.
- PART B, DOC CHANGES: rewrite the pillar 'Children are protected in the fiction' as 'Children are never spectacle': a child can die in SUMUD because children die in Gaza, but the camera keeps the child's frame, the act is never shown, the death is never a mechanic, a twist or a punishment, and it is never preventable by the player. Keep 'never uses a child's death as a twist'. Note This War of Mine's opposite choice (children cannot die, an adult carries them away) so the founder can see why players read that as evasion, and note Liyla so the sensitivity readers have a same-subject precedent to react to. The two Palestinian readers the doc requires should review the Day 7 night scene before any other scene in the game.
