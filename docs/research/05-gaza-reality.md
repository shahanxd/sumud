# gaza-reality

## Summary
Research brief for SUMUD's ten days, built from 45+ pages actually opened (UN OCHA, UN News, UNICEF, WFP, UNESCO, Al Jazeera, Reuters via ToI, AFP via France24, +972, Al-Haq, We Are Not Numbers essays, Hyperallergic, Jerusalem Post, palquest). Headline results: (1) the drone "zanana" is the constant sound bed and, unlike jets, gives no warning before a strike; tank shells and bombs whistle before impact; sonic booms shake buildings and break windows; (2) roof knocking is a pre-2023 practice that Israel itself said on 9 Oct 2023 was "not the system currently applying" — the 2023-25 warnings are leaflets, SMS, recorded voice calls and phone calls; (3) pre-war power was under 7 hours a day (2018), 14-15 hours after Qatari fuel (2019), roughly 12 hours of cuts in summer 2023, then total blackout from 11 Oct 2023, after which life runs on 1-2 shekel phone charging, $420 solar panels and generators giving shelters about two hours; (4) water comes by 10,000-litre truck into 20-litre jerrycans, queued from before dawn, at 6-10 litres per person per day; bread queues start at 5-6 am for a 3-shekel bundle from hand-cranked ovens; (5) UNRWA school shelters hold about 50 people per classroom, women and children inside, men in the playground, tents in the courtyard, and classes resume in tents 8-11 am with cardboard for desks; (6) fishermen are held to 3-6 nm (Oslo said 20), enforced by gunboats with live fire, rubber bullets, arrests and boat seizure, and to ~800 m during the war, while the beach remains "our only refuge" and the site of 6,000- and 13,000-child kite records; (7) documented war weddings keep the embroidered dress, henna, dabke, zaghrouta ululation, songs from a loudspeaker and a decorated tent, and improvise everything else; one Dec 2025 wedding tent was shelled minutes after the party, killing an 8-year-old; (8) language: Gazans colloquially say "al-yahud" for the army/state (BBC's own finding), which is contested and must not be in our script; the safe, true register is "they", "the army" (al-jaysh), "the occupation" (al-ihtilal), "the planes", "the zanana", with Israel named plainly in the title card and sources tab; (9) sumud, the 1948 key and tatreez all have primary or near-primary sourcing (palquest/IPS, New Arab testimony, UNESCO inscription 2021). Pages I could not open (Gisha, UNRWA.org, B'Tselem, Save the Children, NPR, CBC, Electronic Intifada, DCI) are flagged so the parent can retry in a browser.

## Findings
- [verified] 'Zanana' is the Gazan word for the surveillance drone and its buzz; it is a constant presence, day and night, and residents describe it as eating their minds and stealing sleep.
  Fatma Al Zahraa Sehwail (Al Jazeera, 21 Jan 2025) calls the drone 'a continuous companion for us in Gaza for many years'; a displaced woman told her 'The drone is eating my mind', a chronic headache worse at night that brings nightmares; the buzz wrecks nerves, escalates family arguments and disrupts cooking and hygiene. Shahd Safi (We Are Not Numbers, 13 Nov 2023) compares it to 'a mosquito in my ear', 'buzz-saws or motorcycles', sees them as 'two black or brown triangles wandering the sky' and at night 'sparkling and shining like stars and sometimes lit up orange'; her cousin's first observation on reaching Egypt was 'that I wasn't hearing the noisy drones'. Hyperallergic (2025) quotes Muin Abu Amsha: 'The most terrible thing in this war: the sound of the drones. It's with you all night.' Wikipedia (secondary) gives the literal meaning 'buzzing sound/noisemaker', from an Egyptian dialect word for a nagging wife, used by synecdoche for the drone itself and metaphorically for informants. Game use: the zanana is the ambient bed of every siege day and its absence (Day 1-2, and any ceasefire beat) should be audible.

- [verified] A drone strike arrives without warning; warplanes and shells announce themselves. Bombs and tank shells whistle before impact; the blast shakes the house from foundation to roof, a gust of wind follows, the sky glows crimson/orange, windows shatter, the air smells of gunpowder, and families avoid showing light for fear of drones.
  Safi: warplanes 'loudly announce themselves' but 'when a drone strikes, you don't know it's coming. Suddenly something — it could be you — explodes.' Ahmad Abushawish (WANN, 19 Nov 2025) describes bombs and tank shells 'each with distinct whistling sounds before impact', constant drone droning and roaring jets, and planes dropping aid. Rahaf Abuzarifa (WANN, 28 Feb 2024): 'Sixteen consecutive airstrikes', 'a resounding blast', 'The sky glowed crimson', 'the entire house quivered from its foundation to its peak; a gust of wind followed', 'the air reeked of gunpowder', 'shattered glass windows, our collapsed kitchen roof', 'we avoided light for fear of attracting the drones', and she covered her little brother's ears at each strike; shrapnel jammed the wheels of their luggage during evacuation. Safa Joudeh (Gulf News, 28 Dec 2008): heard the first explosion, ran to the window, was 'pushed back by the force and the pressure of another explosion', then glass, then bodies and funerals. Hyperallergic quotes Ahmed Dremly on exploding robots (deep vibration, blast wave from behind, then a sound that 'drowns out thought') and Plestia Alaqad's 'an orchestra outside my window' of bombing, crying children and screaming. The 'silence then ambulances' tail of the sequence in the task prompt was not directly documented in any page I opened; treat it as plausible but unsourced.

- [verified] Remote-detonated 'exploding robots' (armoured carriers packed with explosives, dragged into place by bulldozers) have been used since May 2024 and produce a heavy metallic rumble that levels a whole row of houses.
  Al Jazeera (16 Oct 2025): Um Ahmed al-Dreimli, 50, Gaza City, described 'a heavy metallic rumble' and 'it felt as if the ground was being pulled from under our feet'; Sharif Shadi, 22, Jabalia: 'moments later, everything is reduced into bits of rubble'; Civil Defence spokesman Mahmoud Basal put the kill radius at ~500 m and said one device destroys 'a complete row of 10 adjacent houses' versus 2-3 for an airstrike; Euro-Med counted ~300 units a day destroyed this way before the Oct 2025 ceasefire. Useful as a distinct, documented sound event for a Sumud-act day if the design wants one.

- [verified] F-16 sonic booms over Gaza are experienced as 'a bomb dropped directly over their heads': they shake buildings, break windows and blow off doors, and children respond with sleeplessness, bedwetting and loss of appetite.
  Al Jazeera (2 Jan 2006): residents compare the booms to a bomb overhead; psychiatrist Eyad Sarraj: 'It is so quiet, and suddenly … the sound itself is so loud it is like a thunder ball exactly on top of your head'; children under six cannot distinguish simulated from real bombing; 77 people, mainly children and elderly women, hospitalised after December 2005 booms; five miscarriages. Hyperallergic (2025) cites Steve Goodman's account of 'broken windows, ear pain, nosebleeds, anxiety attacks, sleeplessness' from booms. Al Jazeera (16 Sept 2025): rescuer Bashir Hajjaj lists 'the shelling, the helicopters, the missiles, drones and the F-16 aircraft'. B'Tselem's own page was rate-limited on four attempts and is not cited.

- [verified] 'Roof knocking' (a small warhead-less munition on the roof, or a phone call/SMS, 5-15 minutes before the real strike) is a 2006-2021 practice; on 9 Oct 2023 a senior Israeli source said it 'is not the system currently applying' and would be used only 'in certain circumstances'.
  France24 (20 May 2021): warnings are 'a text message, a phone call, or an initial strike on the roof'; 165,000 phone calls during 2008-09; a UN report found strikes 'only five minutes after the phone call'; resident: 'We received last night a call to evacuate the place, and when we did, the entire neighbourhood was destroyed'; Amnesty: warnings lacked timelines and safe destinations and the knock itself 'could also be lethal'. Times of Israel liveblog (9 Oct 2023): senior Israeli source, roof knocking 'is not the system currently applying'. Wikipedia (secondary) summarises the Goldstone finding that civilians 'cannot be expected to know whether a small explosion is a warning or part of an actual attack'. Design consequence: if the game's siege is meant to read as 2023-25, do not use roof knocks; use leaflets, SMS and recorded calls. If the year stays unstated, a roof knock on Day 3 remains defensible as pre-2023 documented practice, but only once.

- [verified] Pre-war electricity: under 7 hours a day before Oct 2018, 14-15 hours after Qatar-funded fuel (2019), supply meeting less than half of demand; about 12 hours of daily cuts in summer 2023; hospitals ran 13-22 hours with priority lines and rooftop solar.
  OCHA (16 July 2019): three sources (Israeli feeder lines, Gaza Power Plant, Qatar-funded fuel, $60m Oct 2018 + $180m May 2019); nine hospitals went from 13 to 22 hours a day after grid upgrades; Nasser Hospital 250 kWp solar (Japan). Al Jazeera (27 Aug 2023): residents 'endure approximately 12 hours of daily power cuts, particularly during summer demand periods'; a mother of six: 'The sea is our only refuge in Gaza', because outages in the heat drive families to the beach. Wikipedia (secondary) records less than four hours a day in mid-2017. Gisha's '8 hours on, 8 off' schedule appeared only in a search snippet (Gisha returned 403 twice) and is not confirmed here.
  src: https://www.ochaopt.org/content/improvements-gaza-electricity-supply; https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2023/8/27/palestinians-flock-to-gazas-beaches-to-bid-summer-farewell; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_crisis_in_the_Gaza_Strip

- [verified] Since 11 Oct 2023 Gaza has had no grid electricity; life runs on phone-charging stalls (1-4 shekels a charge), scarce solar (~$420 a panel, ~$1,200 a battery, tenfold wartime prices), phone torches for light, and generators that give shelters about two hours.
  Al Jazeera (29 Mar 2026): the power plant shut on 11 Oct 2023 when fuel ran out; Abdel Karim Salman, 28, walks 150-200 m daily to charge phones, pays 2-4 shekels a session (270-300 shekels/month) and lights his tent with phone torches: 'There is no refrigerator, no washing machine … even baby milk cannot be stored'; Jamal Musbah, 50, runs a six-panel charging stall (four panels destroyed when a neighbour's house was hit), 50-60 phones a day at 1-2 shekels; solar lamps rose to 300 shekels. Al Jazeera (27 Oct 2023) shelter resident Noor: 'We sometimes have two hours of electricity, depending on the generators.' Game use: the Day 3+ light economy (battery, generator hours, panel, phone torch) matches the record; a charging-stall neighbour is a documented character type.

- [verified] Water arrives by truck (10,000-15,000 litres) or, when fuel runs out, by donkey cart; families queue from before dawn with 20-litre containers for a ration of 6-10 litres per person per day (pre-war ~80; WHO minimum 50-100), and by noon it is gone.
  UN News (13 Apr 2025): driver Ibrahim Alloush waits ~5 hours at Jabalia's desalination plant to fill, then residents 'line up with containers at the truck'; UNICEF ration 6 litres/person/day for ~1 million people, down from 16, could fall below 4 without fuel; water 90-100 shekels per m3; many 'spend half a day waiting for only 5-10 gallons'. The New Arab (19 May 2026): under 10 litres vs ~80 pre-war, sometimes 2; families 'wake before dawn to secure queue positions', elderly and women make 4-5 refills a day; one man: 'I can barely fill five small containers, each holding 20 litres, for my family of seven … by noon, we are already running low'; a 63-year-old: unfiltered water 'causes stomach pain or diarrhoea among children'. UNICEF (22 Mar 2024): one toilet per 700 people in shelters, showers 'once every two weeks', 5,000 ten-litre jerrycans distributed. Game use: Day 4's water truck puzzle should use 20-litre jerrycans (heavy, two-handed for a child), a queue that forms in the dark, and a truck that runs dry.

- [verified] Bakeries are the street's lifeline: queues start at 5-6 am, a subsidised bundle of 8-9 pitas costs 3 shekels (~$1), hand-cranked ovens make ~30 bundles an hour, and by late 2023 60% of bakeries were out of action with about 40 damaged or destroyed.
  Gulf News (29 Oct 2023): people queue 'from as early as 5:00 am'; one man had visited three bakeries since dawn; bakery association head: '60 percent of businesses were out of action'; WFP 'was relying on 23 bakeries to feed 220,000 people a day... only two are functional'; Suleiman al-Huli's hand-operated bakery 'produces 30 bundles (of pitta) an hour'; ~40 bakeries hit; Mohammed Qaranawi: 'You can wait in line for hours and in the end you don't get a turn to get bread.' WFP (5 May 2026): ~100 registered bakeries pre-war, 26 WFP-supported now supplying a third of bread; 3 shekels a bundle; Abu Tair arrived at 6 am; al-Heloo bakery still rubble; bread 'a staple of Palestinian life, eaten with nearly every meal'. - [verified] The fishing limit: Oslo allowed 20 nautical miles; Israel cut it to 3 nm in late 2008, 6 nm most years, with swings between 3 and 15 nm (changed 14 times in Apr-Oct 2019); during the 2023-25 war boats were held to ~800 m and in Jan 2025 the sea was declared a no-go zone.
  OCHA (19 Nov 2019): 3,617 registered fishers (10,000+ before 2000), ~18,000 dependents; 2019: at least 248 firing incidents, 17 injured (2 children), 33 detained (3 children), 19 boats seized, limit up to 15 nm south and 6 nm north. Al-Haq (8 Dec 2011): limit 3 nm since late 2008; gunboats fire rubber-coated bullets, force fishermen to strip and jump into the water, beat and blindfold them, confiscate boats worth up to $150,000. The New Arab: 6 nm zone fluctuating 3-9; an engine costs ~$9,000; fisherman hit by 23 bullets in Jan 2016; union head Nizar Ayyash: '95% of the fishermen in the Gaza Strip are below the poverty line'. Al Jazeera (20 July 2011): water cannon and live fire beyond 3 nm. Al Jazeera (8 Mar 2026): ~800 m limit 'depending on Israeli naval discretion'; 'it depends on the soldier's mood whether he lets you fish or decides to shoot you dead'; 238 fishermen killed since Oct 2023, 72% of fleet destroyed, output under 7.3% of pre-war. Al Jazeera (6 Nov 2025): Jan 2025 'no-go zone'; 200 fishers killed per Ministry of Agriculture (Dec 2024). ## Recommendations
- Sound: make the zanana the permanent bed of every siege day and cut it entirely on Days 1-2 and in the final beach scene so its absence lands. Give tank shells and bombs a whistle (documented) but give drone strikes none (documented) — that asymmetry is your 'surprise' mechanic without a timed button: sometimes the birds leave the roof, sometimes nothing warns you. A real explosion transient is defensible; the sources describe blast, pressure, a gust of wind, crimson sky and glass, so a full-band impact followed by a long rumble and ringing is truer than a soft thud. Keep the separate rumble bus.
- Warnings: if the ten days are meant to feel like 2023-25, drop roof knocking (Israel said on 9 Oct 2023 it was no longer the norm) and use the documented trio — a leaflet drifting down (use the real leaflet register: 'For your safety, you need to evacuate... immediately and head to known shelters'), an SMS on Karim's phone, and a recorded voice call that Teta hangs up on. If the year stays unstated, one roof knock on Day 3 is still defensible.
- Electricity schedule by day: Days 1-2 roughly half a day of grid power (7-15 h documented pre-war, ~12 h cuts in summer 2023); Day 3 total blackout (11 Oct 2023 precedent); Days 4-10 generator windows of about two hours, a rooftop panel that charges phones and one lamp, and a neighbour who runs a 1-2 shekel charging stall. Show the phone torch as the default light source.
- Water and bread puzzles should use the documented numbers: 20-litre jerrycans, a 10,000-litre truck that serves two camps, a queue that forms before dawn and empties by noon, 6-10 litres per person; bakery queue from 5 am, 3-shekel bundle of 8-9 pitas, a hand-cranked oven making ~30 bundles an hour, children splitting into two lines.
- Shelter (Days 7-10): 50 people to a classroom, women and children inside, men and boys in the courtyard under tarpaulins, laundry on the railings, two hours of generator light, a science room with a hole in the ceiling; lessons in the courtyard from 8 to 11 with cardboard as slates and a very small board. Karim sleeping outside with the men is a documented gender split worth using.
- Sea (Day 6): the limit is enforced by a gunboat silhouette on the horizon, a searchlight, a loudhailer and water cannon before live fire; Baba goes out by day so 'they can see we are only fishing' — write that line, it is verbatim testimony. Gaza City harbour is a documented location; its boats were destroyed in 2023-25.
- Weddings (Days 9-10): keep dress with red tatreez, henna night, zaghrouta, men's dabke, borrowed artificial flowers, a tent or courtyard strung with coloured lights and a gold-framed mirror, sumaqiyya and rice trays, and a hall manager or elder who reads the guest list and notices who is missing. For the halal requirement, the documented war weddings use recorded songs from a loudspeaker; voice-only zaffa chants and hand-clapped dabke rhythms are consistent with what is documented and avoid the instrument question entirely — verify permissibility of the duff with the founder's own scholar, this brief did not research fiqh.
- Language for the script: characters say 'they', 'the army' (al-jaysh), 'the occupation' (al-ihtilal), 'the planes', 'the zanana', 'the tank'. Never write 'al-yahud' even though it is real Gazan speech: the BBC found Gazans often use it for the army and state, a 2013 BBC ruling allowed 'Israeli' as translation, and the 2025 row shows it would be subtitled as 'Jews' by critics and sink the game. Name Israel plainly once — in the title card, the sources tab and the store page ('set in Gaza under Israeli blockade and bombardment') — so the perpetrator is clear and the dialogue stays about bread and school.
- Show the perpetrator without a villain: tank silhouettes at the end of the street (Tal al-Hawa/Daraj precedent), a quadcopter between buildings, F-16 booms that crack a window, a gunboat on the horizon, leaflets, the Al-Rashid coastal road as the Day 7 route, and the corridor checkpoint where soldiers are shadows behind a berm and a loudhailer says 'hands up, phone up, say your name' while the player's hands are full. That satisfies 'not cowards' while keeping the camera on the family.
- Death of a child: the documented cases to model tone on are the 8-year-old nephew killed when a wedding tent was shelled (Dec 2025) and the four-year-old killed after flying a kite (Aug 2026, likely). If Sami dies, let it follow the record — off-screen strike, a friend who stops flying his kite, an adult's responsibility in a child's mouth ('I have no one to cheer for me now') — and never as a twist.
