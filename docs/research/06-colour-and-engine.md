# colour-and-engine

## Summary
Research on two questions for SUMUD (Godot 4, silhouette 2D narrative game, all art from code and shaders). A. Colour: across the nine reference games, the premium look never comes from flat colour itself; it comes either from lighting and atmosphere over simple geometry (Limbo, Inside, Somerville, Badland's fog/glow layers) or from a painter (Badland's mid layer, Gris, Journey's hand-painted shadows, Night in the Woods). The two references closest to a code-only pipeline, Kentucky Route Zero and Alto's, both run flat colour inside a 3D engine and lean on limited per-scene palettes, atmospheric gradients and stage-style lighting rather than surface paint. Honest answer to the founder: yes, SUMUD can and should carry more colour than Limbo, but colour should live in light, sky, sea, haze and a few accent objects (kites, tatreez, bread, fire), never as flat paint on the silhouettes, and it should be dithered, graded and rationed. Flat coded colour reads as cheap exactly when it is large, untextured, banded, fully saturated at mid-brightness, or when many hues compete with clean identical shapes. Concrete plan: Badland's layer model (dark silhouette gameplay layer over a coloured, hazy, lit background) plus Inside's accent rule (one or two saturated accents per scene) plus a Gris-style arc where colour is taken away on Day 3 and earned back by Day 10. B. Engine: Godot 4.7.2 (18 Aug 2026) is the latest stable. All 2D lighting nodes (PointLight2D, DirectionalLight2D, LightOccluder2D, CanvasModulate), 2D glow via WorldEnvironment with 2D HDR on Forward+/Mobile (since 4.2; Compatibility has a reduced glow), Polygon2D/Line2D, GPUParticles2D, headless CLI (--headless, --script, --export-release, --import), GUT 9.7.1 and gdUnit4 6.2.1, Android/iOS export requirements, touch/gamepad input and Arabic text shaping via TextServerAdvanced (HarfBuzz+ICU+Graphite) are all confirmed against the official 4.7 docs. The one caution: Skeleton2D/Bone2D are stable but the whole SkeletonModification2D IK family is still marked Experimental in the 4.7 docs, so IK for the carry animations should be written in GDScript rather than depend on it. Export templates (.tpz) for 4.7.2 are 1.3 GB (mirror listing); 4.7 added a per-platform downloader.

## Findings
- [verified] Limbo is monochrome greyscale with film grain and lighting doing the atmospheric work; its custom engine could only render monochrome, which is why Playdead moved to Unity for Inside.
  Wikipedia's Limbo article states the game uses dark greyscale graphics, film grain and minimal ambient sound, cites film noir and German Expressionism, and says Playdead built a custom engine for Limbo and later switched to Unity 'citing their Limbo engine's limitation to monochromatic visuals'. Team was about 8 core developers (16 with freelancers) over roughly 2.5 years, and about 70% of built content was thrown out. A cinematic-platformers fan site adds that there is no colour accent anywhere and that depth of field is used for a diorama feel (enthusiast source, so 'likely' for those details).
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game); https://cinematicplatformers.com/games/limbo/

- [verified] Inside is real 3D in Unity rendered in a near-monochrome palette with colour used sparingly to highlight the player and select environment parts; its 'high fidelity from simple shapes' comes from lighting, volumetrics and dithering, not from surface detail.
  Wikipedia: Inside was made in Unity, 2.5D, 'the game is dark, with color used sparingly to highlight both the player and certain parts of the environment', and Playdead open-sourced its temporal reprojection anti-aliasing in March 2016. The GDC Vault session page for 'Low Complexity, High Fidelity: INSIDE Rendering' (Mikkel Gjoel, Mikkel Svendsen) lists local shadowed volumetrics, a water renderer, lighting authored as separate diffuse, specular and bounce entities, analytic primitive-based ambient occlusion, screen-space reflections, and 'dithering to get rid of distracting artefacts' (colour banding). Playdead's public publications repo contains rendering_inside_gdc2016.pdf and banding_in_games.pdf (stored as Git LFS pointers, so I could not read the PDFs themselves). The specific 'red shirt' accent appears only in search snippets, not on a page I opened.
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_(video_game); https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023002/Low-Complexity-High-Fidelity-INSIDE; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/video-how-playdead-rendered-the-unique-visuals-of-i-inside-i-; https://github.com/playdeadgames/publications/tree/master/INSIDE

- [verified] Badland's look is a three-layer stack: a blurred tiling colour background, a pure-black silhouette gameplay layer, and ultra-detailed hand-painted mid assets; colour per time of day was set with Photoshop adjustment layers, and low-opacity fog and glow layers are the signature.
  Lead designer/artist Juhana Myllys's own portfolio page describes the combination of 'blurred backgrounds, ultra detailed hand painted assets and almost abstract but still logical pure black gaming layer', influences from Hudson River School painting, animation films and nature photos, Photoshop adjustment layers to find the look of each time of day, and four zones (Dawn, Noon, Dusk, Night) chosen partly so assets could be recycled while the world visibly degrades. He notes fog and glowing 'low opacity layers were used so rarely in 2D games'. This is the closest published model to SUMUD's 'silhouettes over colour', and note that the mid layer was hand-painted, not coded.
  src: https://www.jmyllys.com/badland

- [verified] Somerville is low-poly 3D in Unity with Playdead-style stark lighting; characters are designed as readable silhouettes with negative space, and animation is layered and procedural with IK constraints rather than hand-keyed; reviewers describe drab environment colour against luminous alien light.
  Jumpship's blog states all assets were integrated in Unity, that the team prioritised readable silhouettes at any camera distance ('carving out as much negative space as possible' with costume accents at joints), cites Another World and Flashback, and describes a layered procedural animation approach using IK for head and arms plus emotional-state layers. Wikipedia confirms Unity, Chris Olsen as director/writer, Dino Patti as executive producer, 2014 start and November 2022 release, and mixed reception (Metacritic 72 PC). The Finger Guns review says lighting and shadows feel like Playdead but with more 'scope, colour and direction', with 'drab colours of the apocalyptic environment juxtaposed with the shining and ominous aliens' (review opinion, so the colour characterisation is 'likely').
  src: https://blog.jumpship.co.uk/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somerville_(video_game); https://fingerguns.net/reviews/2023/09/12/somerville-review-ps5-i-want-to-believe/

- [verified] Alto's Adventure and Odyssey are flat minimalist art built by one artist-programmer (Harry Nesbitt) in Unity, with procedurally generated terrain and a dynamic day-night and weather system doing most of the visual variety; the stated philosophy is to simplify each form to its essence and let the player's mind fill in the rest.
  Wikipedia (Alto's Adventure): Unity, procedurally generated landscapes, flat minimalist art, day-night cycle and weather, Snowman (three people) plus Nesbitt. Game Developer's IGF interview quotes Nesbitt on asking 'how far can you simplify a form until you're left only with its essence' and building an 'impression' the player completes, and credits Unity. The Game Developer case study confirms Nesbitt was hired September 2013 as sole artist and developer, the game shipped February 2015, and it includes procedurally generated mountains, weather and time of day. Wikipedia (Odyssey): three biomes designed to play differently, dynamic weather events like sandstorms, a switch to Metal on iOS, a 60 fps target with lower-quality shaders on weak hardware. I could not open Nesbitt's 'Making of Alto's Adventure' blog post (TLS error on both http and https), so the specific claim that skies are pure gradients is observable in the game but not confirmed from a page I read.
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto%27s_Adventure; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto%27s_Odyssey; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-team-alto-s-i-alto-s-odyssey-i-; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/alto-s-adventure-case-study; https://thesweetsetup.com/quick-qa-team-alto-makers-altos-odyssey/

- [verified] Gris is hand-painted watercolour art in Unity where colour is restored chapter by chapter as the emotional arc progresses; the team found the early, colourless areas the hardest to make appealing.
  Wikipedia: Unity engine; Conrad Roset is director and artist; four locations (desert with windmills, forest, underwater caverns, light-based architecture) each restore colour; the team consulted a psychologist on the five stages of grief; because 'the game becomes more lively as players progress', making early areas appealing was difficult and they drew on Iwagumi aquarium composition. Roset, in an interview, confirms 'hand-drawn watercolor animations' and says 'aesthetics and mechanics, and the relationship between them, play a powerful role in how we tell the story, as we eliminate all other elements.' A fan analysis maps the order as white/grey (denial), red desert (anger), green forest (bargaining), blue underwater (depression), yellow sky city (acceptance), which matches Wikipedia's location order; the grief mapping is secondary analysis, so 'likely'.
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gris; https://thatgamersasylum.wordpress.com/2019/01/26/gris-creative-director-conrad-roset-interview/; https://calxylian.com/gris-the-philosophy-of-colors/

- [verified] Journey's palette moves from golden desert to blues and whites and finally snow as the player climbs; the art was made by removing everything that did not serve the emotion, with shadows hand-painted rather than computed.
  Wikipedia: PhyreEngine; the environment 'employs a dynamic color progression: golden desert tones transition to blues and whites as the player ascends the mountain, culminating in snow-covered peaks'; designed 'like a Japanese garden' by removing elements that did not fit; team grew from 7 to 18 over more than three years and the studio was driven to bankruptcy in the final months. Game Developer's write-up of Matt Nava's talk: thatgamecompany 'tried to do the most with the least', Nava hand-painted all shadows and aligned shadow textures to the pixel grid for the sunset columns. Relevance: a tiny palette can carry a whole game if each chapter owns one hue family, but Journey's polish came from three years and a painter, not from code.
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game); https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/journeys-art-director-just-broke-down-the-title-s-timeless-aesthetic-in-glorious-detail

- [verified] Kentucky Route Zero is flat vertex-coloured low-poly 3D geometry built in Blender and Unity, staged like theatre with stage lighting and slow-cinema framing; this is the closest published model to 'geometry plus flat colour plus lights' that reads as premium.
  Tamas Kemenczy's Uses This interview: KRZ is developed in Unity Pro with C#, Blender is his main graphics tool with custom Python scripts 'mainly for working with obsolete vertex-based coloring and line art', GIMP for textures, Grapher for shader and procedural curves. Wikipedia: Unity; the team studied theatre scripts, which shaped 'environment design and treatment of space, lighting and movement'. The GDC 2014 talk 'Scenography of Kentucky Route Zero' is described as covering 'the use of theatrical stagecraft and slow cinema' and how the game 'balances mood, realism and the mundane in its set pieces'. Takeaway for SUMUD: flat colour works when the scene is lit like a stage (dark surround, one or two coloured lights, strong composition), which is exactly what Godot's 2D lights and CanvasModulate provide.
  src: https://usesthis.com/interviews/tamas.kemenczy/; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Route_Zero; https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020596/Scenography-of-Kentucky-Route

- [likely] Night in the Woods is flat vector 2D art in Unity with an autumn palette, drawn by a self-taught animator (Scott Benson) plus a hired animator; it won Best 2D Visuals at the Unity Awards 2017. The exact vector tool used is not confirmed.
  Wikipedia: Unity; Scott Benson lead artist; flat vector-based 2D look; autumn setting drives the palette; Charles Huettner hired as animator after the Kickstarter exceeded its goal; Best 2D Visuals, Unity Awards 2017. The Cliqist Q&A and the search results do not state whether Flash/Animate or Illustrator was used, so tooling is unverified. Relevance: NITW's flat style depends on a distinctive hand-drawn character vocabulary and dense per-scene detail, which is the part a code-only pipeline cannot cheaply reproduce.
  src: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_in_the_Woods; https://cliqist.com/2017/03/29/night-in-the-woods-curious-cat-qa-scott-benson/

- [verified] Honest answer on colour for a code-only pipeline: colour should be carried by light and atmosphere, not by painted surfaces; the failure mode of flat coded colour is large untextured fills, banded gradients, saturated mid-tone hues, competing palettes and clean repeated shapes.
  Synthesis of the above. Every reference that reads as premium has either (a) lighting/atmosphere over simple geometry (Limbo's grain and light, Inside's volumetrics and dithering, Somerville's stark lights, Badland's fog and glow layers, KRZ's stage lighting) or (b) a painter (Badland's mid layer, Gris, Journey's hand-painted shadows, NITW). None of the flat-colour games ship large flat fills without atmosphere: Alto's uses time-of-day gradients and weather, KRZ uses darkness and coloured stage lights. Playdead's GDC session explicitly names colour banding as the artefact that breaks a dark, gradient-heavy image, so any coded sky or sea gradient must be dithered (Godot: a screen-space noise/blue-noise dither in a post shader). Godot's 2D antialiasing page confirms that geometry edges on Line2D/Polygon2D show 'staircase' aliasing without MSAA 2D or the antialiased flag, which is the other giveaway of cheap vector art.
  src: https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023002/Low-Complexity-High-Fidelity-INSIDE; https://www.jmyllys.com/badland; https://usesthis.com/interviews/tamas.kemenczy/; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_antialiasing.html

- [verified] 2D lighting nodes are all present and documented for 4.7: PointLight2D (shape from a texture), DirectionalLight2D (sun/moon; shadows are infinitely long), LightOccluder2D (polygon occluders, also inside TileMapLayer), CanvasModulate (ambient base tint, one per canvas), with PCF5/PCF13 shadow filters and per-light cull masks.
  The 4.7 '2D lights and shadows' tutorial states: PointLight2D is omnidirectional/spot with texture, offset, texture_scale and height (for normal mapping); DirectionalLight2D has height and max_distance and its shadows remain infinitely long; LightOccluder2D occluders 'can be placed as independent nodes or can be part of a TileMapLayer node'; CanvasModulate sets the unlit base colour and without it scenes look overly bright; shadow filter PCF13 'should only be used for a few lights at once due to its high rendering cost'; both light types have Item Cull Mask and Occluder Light Mask; larger lights cost more, and additive-blend sprites are a cheaper alternative when no shadows are needed. The PointLight2D class page says 'This light's shape is defined by a (usually grayscale) texture' and lists inherited Light2D energy, color, blend_mode, range layers/z, cull masks and shadow properties. The CanvasModulate class page: 'Only one can be used to tint a canvas, but CanvasLayers can be used to render things independently.'
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_lights_and_shadows.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_pointlight2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_canvasmodulate.html

- [verified] 2D glow works through WorldEnvironment; since Godot 4.2, 2D HDR rendering (Forward+ and Mobile only) lets Modulate values above 1.0 drive glow; without HDR you set background mode to Canvas and lower Glow HDR Threshold; the Compatibility renderer uses a reduced glow with several properties hidden.
  The 'Environment and post-processing' page: 'Since Godot 4.2, developers can enable HDR for 2D rendering when using the Forward+ and Mobile rendering methods' to control which objects glow via Modulate; for performance with HDR off, 'set the environment background mode to Canvas then decrease Glow HDR Threshold' and keep UI in a CanvasLayer; 'when using the Compatibility rendering method, glow uses a different implementation with some properties being unavailable' (Levels, Normalized, Strength, Blend Mode). The Godot 4.2 release article: 'For Forward+ and Mobile rendering methods this release introduces 2D HDR rendering, unlocking 3D effects, such as glow, for 2D games.' Implication for SUMUD's kites, candles and windows: use Forward+ on desktop, Mobile renderer for phones; do not plan the look around Compatibility.
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html; https://godotengine.org/article/godot-4-2-arrives-in-style/

- [verified] Polygon2D and Line2D cover all vector geometry needs: per-vertex colours for gradients, textures with UVs, skeleton weights, invert mode, width curves, gradients, joint/cap modes and closed loops; MSAA 2D is Forward+/Mobile only, and the docs contradict themselves on whether Polygon2D has an antialiased flag.
  Polygon2D class page: 'If true, polygon edges will be anti-aliased' (antialiased); 'Color for each vertex. Colors are interpolated between vertices, resulting in smooth gradients' (vertex_colors); texture + uv per vertex; skeleton NodePath with bone weights, internal_vertex_count, invert_enabled/invert_border. Line2D class page: points, width, width_curve, gradient, default_color, joint_mode (sharp/bevel/round), begin/end cap modes, texture and texture_mode, round_precision, closed, antialiased, with the caveat 'Line2D is not accelerated by batching when being anti-aliased'. The 4.2 release added Line2D.closed. The 2D antialiasing tutorial says MSAA 2D (Rendering > Anti Aliasing > Quality > MSAA 2D) 'is only available in the Forward+ and Mobile renderers, not the Compatibility renderer', does not fix aliasing from custom 2D shaders or Light2D specular, and states 'Polygon2D, TextureProgressBar, and several custom drawing methods don't feature an antialiased property', which conflicts with the Polygon2D class reference; treat it as something to test on day one (expect MSAA 2D to be the reliable path).
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_polygon2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_line2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/2d_antialiasing.html; https://godotengine.org/article/godot-4-2-arrives-in-style/

- [verified] Skeleton2D and Bone2D with Polygon2D weight painting are stable in 4.7, but every IK class (SkeletonModificationStack2D and the SkeletonModification2D family: CCDIK, FABRIK, Jiggle, LookAt, PhysicalBones, StackHolder, TwoBoneIK) is marked Experimental and 'may be changed or removed in future versions'.
  The 4.7 SkeletonModification2D class page carries the Experimental banner and lists the seven subclasses; SkeletonModificationStack2D is likewise marked experimental (enabled defaults to false, modification_count, strength 0..1, and a note that order matters for full-body IK). The Skeleton2D class page (4.7) documents get/set_modification_stack and execute_modifications with no deprecation note. The '2D skeletons' tutorial covers Polygon2D binding, the UV editor for bone weights and internal vertices, does not mention IK, and shows a banner that it is 'not yet updated for Godot 4.7 and may be outdated'. Recommendation: keep Skeleton2D/Bone2D for the rigs, but implement the two-bone IK the carry system needs in GDScript (analytic two-bone solve is a few lines) so the game does not depend on an experimental API during a 2-3 month production.
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonmodification2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeletonmodificationstack2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_skeleton2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/animation/2d_skeletons.html

- [verified] GPUParticles2D is the recommended 2D particle node (ParticleProcessMaterial or ShaderMaterial, trails, sub-emitters, LightOccluder2D collision, visibility rect, interpolation, fixed FPS); emit_particle() needs Forward+/Mobile; CPUParticles2D is a near-parity fallback; 2D particles lack physics interpolation.
  GPUParticles2D class page (4.7): amount and lifetime set emission rate, process_material accepts ParticleProcessMaterial or ShaderMaterial, texture defaults to 1x1 squares, trail_enabled with sections and lifetime, sub_emitter, visibility_rect, interpolate, fixed_fps, fract_delta, collision_base_size for LightOccluder2D collisions, draw_order, local_coords, and 'emit_particle() only works with Forward+ and Mobile renderers, not Compatibility'. The 2D particle systems tutorial recommends GPUParticles2D, says CPUParticles2D has 'near-feature parity ... but lower performance when using large amounts of particles', and notes (as of the 4.3 text) that physics interpolation is not supported for 2D particles. Dust, smoke, sea haze and strike debris for SUMUD are all covered.
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gpuparticles2d.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/particle_systems_2d.html

- [verified] Headless command line is fully supported: --headless equals '--display-driver headless --audio-driver Dummy'; -s/--script runs a GDScript that extends SceneTree or MainLoop; --export-release/--export-debug <preset> <path> export using names from export_presets.cfg; --import imports resources then quits; --headless is required on machines without a GPU.
  The 4.7 command line tutorial documents: '--headless' (combines '--display-driver headless --audio-driver Dummy', useful for servers and CI); '-s, --script <script>' with the note that scripts must inherit SceneTree or MainLoop (example 'godot -s sayhello.gd'); '--export-release <preset> <path>' and '--export-debug <preset> <path>' with the example 'godot --headless --export-release "Linux/X11" /var/builds/project'; the preset name must match export_presets.cfg and needs quotes if it has spaces; '--headless' is 'required on platforms that do not have GPU access (such as continuous integration)'; '--import' starts the editor, waits for imports, then quits; '--quit' exits after the first iteration; '--path <directory>'. The exporting-projects page adds '--export-pack'. Community practice for CI (GUT article, 'Run automated tests for your Godot game on CI') is a clean 'godot --headless --import --quit' before running tests, matching the docs.
  src: https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/editor/command_line_tutorial.html; https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_projects.html

## Recommendations
- Tell the founder the truth: yes, add more colour than Limbo, but as light and atmosphere, never as paint on the silhouettes. Adopt Badland's layer model for SUMUD: (1) a coloured, hazy, dithered sky/sea background that owns most of the hue; (2) five to seven silhouette parallax layers rendered near-black to deep indigo with noise-roughened edges and fog between them (each layer lifted a little toward the sky colour, which sells depth for free); (3) a small set of accent objects rendered in flat saturated colour: kites, tatreez, bread crust, fire, the wedding dress. This is Inside's 'colour sparingly to highlight the player and what matters' rule applied on top of Badland's stack.
- Ration hues per scene: two hue families plus one accent. Skies: dawn amber-to-rose, noon pale cyan-to-white, dusk rose-to-violet, night indigo-to-black; sea: a two-stop gradient a half-step cooler than the sky with light specular sparkle; interiors at night: one warm PointLight2D (amber, Add blend) against a cool CanvasModulate base, which is the candle-versus-blackout contrast the whole light-and-power system is built on. Never light a scene with more than two hues or the coded look collapses into clip art.
- Kill the three giveaways of cheap coded colour on day one of the prototype: (a) banding, with a blue-noise or triangular dither in the final post shader on every gradient (this is what Playdead's INSIDE talk names as the artefact that ruins dark gradient images); (b) staircase edges, by running Forward+ with MSAA 2D and testing whether Polygon2D.antialiased actually works in 4.7 (the class reference says it exists, the antialiasing tutorial says it does not); (c) sameness, by never instancing an identical building or window twice, using seeded jitter on every generated silhouette, and adding the paper grain, ink-bleed and vignette passes already in the doc so flat colour reads as printed, not rendered.
- Make colour the arc of the ten days, subtracting then restoring rather than Gris's monotonic addition: Days 1-2 are the most coloured (gold dawn, blue sea, rose dusk, kites at full saturation); Day 3's first strike collapses the world to grey plus fire orange; Days 4-7 are desaturated, with colour appearing only where care happens (the oven glow, the candle, the water in the jerrycan) and Day 7's walk to the school nearly monochrome; Days 8-10 restore hue in step with the player's remembered acts (the generator light on Day 8, the dress on Day 9), and the Day 10 sky of kites is the single most saturated frame of the game. Store the per-day palette as data (sky stops, sea stops, ambient CanvasModulate colour, accent list) so the arc is one JSON file the founder can tune.
- Use the Godot 2D lighting stack exactly as documented: CanvasModulate for the base ambient (one per canvas, so put UI on its own CanvasLayer), DirectionalLight2D for sun/moon with shadows only on the hero layer, PointLight2D with grayscale textures for windows, candles and the oven (Add blend, PCF5 shadows, PCF13 reserved for one or two hero lights), LightOccluder2D polygons generated with the silhouettes, and 2D HDR plus WorldEnvironment glow for kites, fire and the Day 10 sky. Build on Forward+ for Steam and the Mobile renderer for the later phone build; do not design the look around the Compatibility renderer, which has reduced glow, no MSAA 2D and no emit_particle().
- Keep Skeleton2D/Bone2D rigs with Polygon2D weight painting for the characters, but do not depend on SkeletonModification2D for IK: every IK class is still marked Experimental in the 4.7 docs. Write a small analytic two-bone IK in GDScript for arms (carry poses, kite string hand) and legs (foot planting on rubble), driven by the carry-weight state. This removes the biggest engine-API risk from a 2-3 month schedule.
- Set up the code-only pipeline the doc promises around the verified CLI: 'godot --headless --import --quit' on every commit, then gdUnit4 (v6.2.1, needs Godot 4.5+) for unit tests and scene-runner bot playthroughs (it can simulate keyboard, mouse and touch), producing JUnit XML for CI, and 'godot --headless --export-release "Windows Desktop" build/sumud.exe' for nightly builds. GUT 9.7.1 is an acceptable alternative; pick one, not both. Note gdUnit4 exit codes 0/100/101 versus GUT's 0/1 when wiring CI.
- Download plan for this machine: the 86 MB Windows editor zip plus only the Windows (and later Linux/macOS) export templates through 4.7's per-platform template downloader, instead of the 1.3 GB all-platform .tpz. Add Android templates only when the phone build starts.
- Mobile later is realistic but has hard requirements: Android needs OpenJDK 17+, SDK Platform 35, Build-Tools 35.0.1, Platform-Tools 35.0.0+, CMake 3.10.2.4988404, NDK r28b, plus a Gradle build for an AAB on Google Play (4.7's GABE simplifies standalone export); iOS needs a Mac with Xcode, an App Store Team ID and a bundle identifier, and the exported .xcodeproj is built in Xcode, so budget for a rented Mac or a friend with one. Design the control scheme now so it maps cleanly to 4.7's VirtualJoystick plus TouchScreenButtons, and keep emulate_touch_from_mouse on in a debug profile to test touch on the Windows machine.
- Arabic: keep TextServerAdvanced (default), bundle Noto Naskh Arabic and Amiri as the project fonts because the default font has no Arabic glyphs, rely on the automatic RTL mirroring for Controls, and do not enable 'Include Text Server Data' (it is for dictionary-based line breaking, not Arabic). Put the Quran/hadith cards on a CanvasLayer with their own font sizes and test bidirectional lines that mix Arabic with Latin numerals early, since those are the cases the docs flag.
- Two honest caveats to carry into the plan: Badland's premium mid layer and Gris's colour were painted by hand, so SUMUD's coded version will succeed or fail on lighting, fog and restraint, not on how much colour is added; and the reference that looks most like 'geometry plus flat colour' (Kentucky Route Zero) is staged like theatre in near-darkness with one or two coloured lights per scene, which argues for keeping the doc's scarce-light philosophy and adding colour inside the light rather than lifting the whole image.
