## Etiquette notes
- Match the verse to what is literally on screen. islamqa 175174 (verses in video clips) permits pairing recitation with scenes of Allah's creation when 'these worldly things that are shown in the clip are exactly what is mentioned in the verses' (sea, ships, hunger, homes), and warns against showing worldly scenes under verses about the unseen, e.g. 'it is not appropriate to show worldly gardens and then quote verses that mention the garden of Paradise'. So 16:14 over the sea and 76:8 over bread are ideal; 3:169-170 (martyrs alive with their Lord) should sit over a name on a wall or the dusk sea, never over a depiction of Paradise.
- No music, no vocal or sound effects under any Quran. islamqa 271405 says it is 'not permissible to use them as background to recitation of the Quran, because that is contrary to the required respect and veneration of the Quran', and vocal effects processed until 'they resemble singing and sound like musical instruments' fall under the ruling on music; effects even in the gaps between recitation are 'at the very least... disliked (makruh)'. islamqa 102874 adds that the Quran 'is too great to be recited alongside music' and should not be mixed with jokes or used 'to fill empty time'. Implementation: when a recited ayah plays, duck the ambient bed to silence (or leave only natural sound such as wind and sea, which 271405 does not restrict), and never trigger recitation during a comedic beat.
- Recitation may run while the player is not attending, but nothing may drown it out. islamqa 174743: 'there is nothing wrong with that, so long as there is nothing happening at the same time that would drown it out', and Ibn Baz advised not leaving it on where people are 'engaging in idle talk and speaking and arguing'. Design consequence: recitation belongs in sit-spots and cutscenes where the player cannot move or trigger dialogue, not as a looping background layer over gameplay.
- Do not paint verses onto the silhouette walls as set dressing. islamqa 264601 (verses on walls) rules it makruh and an innovation, because reminding 'is done by spoken words, not by writing the verses', it exposes the words of Allah to disrespect, and people end up gossiping beneath verses that forbid gossip. Verses on the game's environment (bakery wall, rubble, kites) should be avoided; the 1948 key and embroidery carry the visual language instead.
- On-screen text of a verse is fine if it is a reminder, legible, and unornamented. islamqa 300101 (verses as a phone/computer background): 'There is nothing wrong with putting Quranic verses as a background on the screen of a mobile phone, computer and the like', on condition that it is for reminding and reflection rather than decoration, in clear legible Uthmani script, never shaped into animals or ornamental forms (Ibn Uthaymin: 'toying and tampering with the words of Allah'), and with nothing prohibited alongside it. For the game: use a clean Uthmani-style font (KFGQPC Uthmanic Script HAFS or quran.com's script), never bend Arabic into a kite shape or embroidery motif, and keep the frame free of anything haram at that moment.
- Give the reference under every quote and never trim in a way that changes meaning. Print the surah:ayah or collection and number exactly as quran.com and sunnah.com print them (the items above carry those), and if only one clause is shown (e.g. 'Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakeel' from 3:173, or the final sentence of Bukhari 1283) show a complete clause, not a fragment mid-phrase. The Saheeh International brackets are the translator's glosses; keep them or drop them consistently, do not rewrite them.
- The wedding may have a duff and voices, and nothing else. Sunan Ibn Majah 1896 (Hasan) makes the duff and raised voices the mark of a lawful wedding; Sahih al-Bukhari 5147 shows little girls beating the duff and singing before the Prophet. islamqa 20406 and 152009 add the conditions: the duff at weddings, Eid and the return of a traveller; a duff 'that has jangling pieces of metal around it (i.e., tambourine)' is not allowed; and on that site's view the duff is for women and girls, 'it is not permissible to beat the daff except for women'. So the Day 10 sound design is women's and girls' voices, hand-drum without jingles, clapping, dabke footfalls, and no other instrument.
- Wedding and rooftop songs must stay within bounds. In Sahih al-Bukhari 5147 the Prophet let the girls keep singing but stopped the one line that claimed knowledge of the unseen ('Leave this (saying) and keep on saying the verses which you had been saying before'). islamqa 20406 says the daff must not accompany words that 'call for or praise anything that is haram'. Write Teta's songs and the wedding song about home, sea, bread and family; no lines that praise haram things or make theological claims.
- Tears are sunnah, wailing is not. In Sahih al-Bukhari 1284 the Prophet wept over a dying child and called the tears 'mercy which Allah has lodged in the hearts of His slaves'; the chapter heading on the same page concerns punishment connected with wailing (niyaha) by relatives. Show grief as silent tears, held hands and the words of 2:156, not as screaming or self-striking.
- Do not put Quran or hadith in the mouth of a comic beat or a failure state. islamqa 102874 stresses the Quran must be 'respected and guarded against anything inappropriate such as mixing it with jokes'. Never show a verse on the dust-white-out checkpoint screen or next to any UI that can be spammed, skipped with a button mash, or overlaid by a humorous line; reserve it for scripted moments the player cannot interrupt.
- Making the game itself is fine on islamqa's own terms. islamqa 98769 (programming electronic games): 'There is nothing wrong with making the programs that adhere to the shar'i guidelines', which it lists as no nakedness, gambling, witchcraft or music, no crosses or myths, and content directed 'towards sublime goals, such as instilling decent values and morals'. SUMUD's no-music, no-combat, care-based design already meets that list; keep the female characters' depiction modest in silhouette and keep any wedding scene free of mixed dancing.
- Depiction caveat from islamqa 175174 that the studio must decide on: the same answer that permits verses over scenes also says 'the clip should not show any women'. The game has women as central characters, so verses will inevitably appear on screen in scenes with female silhouettes. A workable approach consistent with the reasoning of that answer is to keep the verse cards on their own full-frame moments (sea, sky, bread, the wall of names, an empty street) rather than composited over a character close-up, and to have a scholar or the sensitivity readers sign off on that pattern before launch. This is an inference from the ruling, not something the page says about games.
