# critic_v2_39fa54dae49

## Verdict
The go-to-market section is directionally sound (Steam first, free demo, mobile deliberately last, a fixed charity share, and the kite campaign as a marketing feature) but its calendar and expectations do not survive contact with verified facts. Steam Next Fest cannot happen on the founder's December 2026 launch: the October 2026 edition closed registration on 31 August and the February 2027 edition only admits games still unreleased at the time (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026october, https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration). "Covered by every outlet" is contradicted by the closest comparables, which landed at roughly 130 to 1,000 Steam reviews despite BAFTA wins and Washington Post praise. The founder's "100%" reading of the charity pledge is a tax and consumer-law trap for an Indian sole developer; the GDD's own "fixed share of net revenue" wording is the right one and should be kept, made precise, and backed by a written agreement with the named charity. Steam itself is not the policy risk: Valve's stated rule is to allow everything except illegal content or trolling, and the only Palestine-themed removal on record was UK-only and government-requested. The real policy exposure comes from the founder's new asks (showing the perpetrator, showing child deaths, AI voices, famous reciters' recordings) and from user-generated kites shipping inside the build, none of which the GTM section yet addresses. Fix the calendar this week, keep the charity pledge as a defined percentage, and treat authorship (an Indian founder with no Palestinian collaborators yet) as a first-order marketing risk, not a footnote.

## Top issues
- [blocker] Steam Next Fest is impossible on the stated timeline, and the plan hinges on it
  why: Next Fest October 2026 runs 19 to 26 October and registration closed on 31 August (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026october). The February 2027 edition runs 22 February to 1 March with registration closing 10 January, and Valve's rule is that a game must still be unreleased and may join only one Next Fest ever (https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration). A game that launches at the end of December 2026 therefore never gets a Next Fest. Zukowski's survey of 174 games also shows the fest only pays off above about 2,000 pre-fest wishlists (median gain 322 below 1,000 wishlists, 1,006 in the 1,000 to 9,999 band), and demos launched within a month of the fest gained 2.5x fewer wishlists (https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/, https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/).
  fix: Decide this week between two calendars. Option A: launch in March 2027 after the February Next Fest, with the demo live on its own Steam demo page by early January and registration filed by 10 January. Option B: keep December, drop Next Fest from the plan, and replace it with a standalone Steam demo page, an itch.io mirror, a Steam Playtest (needs no demo, does not touch wishlists, can run before release: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest), and submissions to the non-Valve Steam festivals listed at https://howtomarketagame.com/festivals. Whichever is chosen, rewrite the roadmap gate 'Polish and demo (Next Fest)' accordingly.

- [blocker] The founder's 'yes, 100%' must be read as an emphatic yes, not as 100 percent of revenue; a 100 percent pledge is unworkable and legally exposed
  why: Steam keeps 30 percent of the first 10 million dollars (https://variety.com/2018/gaming/news/valve-revenue-split-changes-1203078700/). Steam classifies developer payments as copyright royalties and, for an Indian payee, US withholding is 15 percent under the treaty (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/taxfaq, https://www.winvesta.in/blog/investors/w-8ben-and-w-8ben-e-form-guide-for-indians-2025-winvesta). Whatever arrives is taxable business income in India, and Section 80G gives no deduction for donations to foreign charities and no deduction at all under the new tax regime (https://cleartax.in/s/donation-under-section-80g-and-80gga, https://www.bankbazaar.com/tax/deduction-under-section-80g.html). Sending the money out is allowed under LRS as 'gift or donation' up to USD 250,000 per year, but 20 percent TCS applies above 10 lakh rupees and must be reclaimed later (https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=115, https://razorpay.com/blog/outward-remittance-limit-india-tcs-rules/). So 'all revenue to charity' means the founder pays income tax out of pocket on money given away, before refunds and chargebacks. Separately, saying on a store page that purchases benefit a charity makes the seller a 'commercial participator' in the UK, requiring a written agreement with the charity and a solicitation statement naming the charity and the amount (Charities Act 1992 Part II: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/41/part/II, https://www.fundraisingregulator.org.uk/about-fundraising/resources/guidance-charitable-institutions-working-commercial-participators), and seven US states require commercial co-venturer registration or contracts, with vague 'a portion of proceeds' wording non-compliant (https://engageforgood.com/do-good-and-sell-it-well-an-overview-of-cause-marketing-regulation/, https://labyrinthinc.com/cause-marketing-commercial-co-venture-guide-massachusetts/).
  fix: Keep the GDD wording and make it precise: 'X percent of the developer's net receipts from Valve (after Steam's share, withholding, refunds and chargebacks) goes to [named charity]'. Copy the precedent wording that has sat on Steam since 2015: '100% of 11bit's proceeds from the DLC go to the charity War Child' (https://store.steampowered.com/app/348040/This_War_of_Mine_War_Child_Charity/). Name the charity now and get its written consent before the page goes live; candidates with verifiable status are Medical Aid for Palestinians (UK charity 1045315, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Aid_for_Palestinians) and Palestine Children's Relief Fund (US 501(c)(3), Charity Navigator 4 stars, https://www.pcrf.net/). Replace 'audited publicly once a year' with a quarterly post showing the Valve payout statement and the charity's receipt; an audit costs money the project does not have. Book one hour with an Indian chartered accountant before the store page is submitted. If the founder wants a way for supporters to give more, add a supporter DLC with three price tiers, exactly the War Child pattern.

- [major] 'Covered by every outlet, streamed by every narrative-game creator' is not supported by any comparable
  why: Liyla and the Shadows of War (2016) was free, won the IMGA storytelling award and got worldwide press, yet its developer could not fund a second game for years and opened a nut roastery (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liyla_and_the_Shadows_of_War, https://hyphenonline.com/2025/06/11/palestinian-video-game-nakba-dreams-on-a-pillow-gaza/). Path Out sits at about 192 Steam reviews, 91 percent positive (https://store.steampowered.com/app/725980/Path_Out/). Bury Me, My Love won a BAFTA category and was called one of the best story games by the Washington Post and has about 129 Steam reviews at 73 percent (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_Me,_My_Love, https://store.steampowered.com/app/808090/Bury_Me_My_Love/). 1979 Revolution: Black Friday took four years, a 304,741 dollar Kickstarter and a Metacritic 80, and has about 1,022 Steam reviews and an estimated 37,000 Steam copies (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Revolution:_Black_Friday, https://steamspy.com/app/388320). This War of Mine's 9 million copies is a studio outlier (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_War_of_Mine). The category is alive: Zukowski counted narrative as the number one genre by games passing 1,000 reviews in 2025 (51 games; 32 excluding Chinese FMV) (https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/), but the games closest to SUMUD cluster at 100 to 1,000 reviews.
  fix: Rewrite 'Why this will sell' with those numbers and a base case of roughly 200 to 1,500 reviews-equivalent. Build the press list from outlets that actually covered Dreams on a Pillow: The Guardian, Polygon, Al Jazeera, PC Gamer, Eurogamer, GamesIndustry, IGN Africa, Kotaku, The National and Hyphen (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_on_a_Pillow, https://kotaku.com/upcoming-stealth-game-dreams-on-a-pillow-palestine-beauty-2000697109). Pitch the kite campaign and the 'street that refuses to stop being a street' angle as the story; 'a Gaza game' alone is not news since Liyla (2016).

- [major] A three-month audience build from zero cannot reach the thresholds the plan implicitly assumes
  why: Mechanics alone eat the runway: a 30-day wait after paying the 100 dollar Steam Direct fee (https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect; the onboarding doc currently says 21 days: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding), a Coming Soon page visible for at least two weeks, and 3 to 5 business days of page review (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/releasing). Zukowski's health check is 150 wishlists in the first two weeks; a part-time solo horror dev who did everything right got 148, from press (45), Twitter (40) and Reddit (35) (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/shy-of-150-a-part-time-solo-dev-s-first-two-weeks-of-collecting-steam-wishlists-). Popular Upcoming has historically needed about 7,000 wishlists and Valve raised the bar in June 2026 (https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/09/26/how-many-wishlists-should-i-have-when-i-launch-my-game/). Standard guidance is a store page 6 to 12 months before launch (https://presskit.gg/field-guides/indie-game-marketing-timeline).
  fix: Pay the fee and submit the store page this week so the clock starts; launch the kite site on the same day the page goes public. Plan on 1,000 to 3,000 wishlists at a December launch, not Popular Upcoming. Follow the zero-following playbook: submit to every relevant Steam festival, email creators and press directly with a demo key and an audience-fit pitch, and skip devlogs and social-following building, which Zukowski's data shows are time sinks (https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/07/15/can-you-market-a-game-with-zero-following/). Track wishlists weekly against the 150-in-two-weeks benchmark and change the capsule and trailer if it misses.

- [major] The founder's content changes (show the perpetrator, show child deaths) silently break the rating, streamer and mobile assumptions in the GTM section
  why: Steam is not the obstacle: Valve's rule is 'allow everything onto the Steam Store, except for things that we decide are illegal, or straight up trolling' (https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2018/jun/07/valve-says-it-will-allow-all-games-in-its-steam-st/), the onboarding list bans hate speech but not political themes (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding), and even Fursan al-Aqsa, a shooter recreating 7 October, was removed only from the UK store after a Counter-Terrorism Internet Referral Unit request, with Valve saying it has to comply with regional authorities; it is still sold in most countries, restricted in Germany and Australia on age-rating grounds (https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2024/11/28/computer-game-recreating-october-7-hamas-attack-pulled-after-police-appeal). The mobile door, however, closes harder: Apple guideline 1.1.2 says enemies 'cannot solely target a specific race, culture, real government, corporation, or any other real entity' and 1.1.7 bars content that seeks 'to profit on recent or current events, such as violent conflicts' (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); Google Play's Sensitive Events policy bans 'appearing to profit from a sensitive event with no discernible benefit to the victims', with an exception for educational, documentary or artistic content (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9878810). The 'PEGI 12, one off-screen death' line also fails if children's deaths are shown: PEGI 12 covers non-realistic violence to human-like characters, realistic death pushes to 16 (https://pegi.info/what-do-the-labels-mean). 11 bit deliberately made children un-killable in The Little Ones as 'a line the developers didn't want to cross' (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/placing-kid-characters-in-a-tragic-situation-in-i-this-war-of-mine-i-).
  fix: Keep 'the enemy has no face' and 'no child death as a twist' as the launch defaults and make any change a vertical-slice decision taken with the sensitivity readers, not a founder note. If the founder insists on showing soldiers or child deaths, re-target PEGI 16, re-check every streamer's willingness to cover it, and accept that Apple and Google become a fight later. The charity share and the sources tab are what would make Google's 'benefit to the victims' and 'documentary or artistic' exceptions arguable, so keep both on the store page from day one.

- [major] The kite campaign ships user-generated names and dedications inside the game with no moderation plan
  why: Every dedicated kite is packed into the build and rises in the Day 10 sky, so one kite named for a militant group, a slur, or a real dead child without family consent ends up in every stream, screenshot and review. Steam's own list bans 'hate speech' and 'content you don't own or have adequate rights to' in what you publish (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/onboarding), and this is the one place SUMUD could actually trip a Steam rule.
  fix: Pattern generator only, no free-drawn images. Dedications limited to a first name plus a short allow-listed phrase set in English and Arabic. Human pre-moderation of every kite before it is packed; freeze the set per build and publish the rules and a takedown route on the kite page. Do not accept full real names unless the submitter attests to being family. Budget the moderation time in the production plan; it is not zero.

- [major] AI voices, generative music and famous recitations are policy and reputation risks the GTM section does not mention
  why: Steam requires a store-page disclosure for any generative AI content that ships in the game, including audio and voice; the January 2026 rewrite exempted only development tools such as code assistants (https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/, https://legalmoveslawfirm.com/steam-ai-policy/, https://www.aitrace.org/company/valve/practice/e62f0dfa-ea81-47ca-98d5-ffbab5fd6814). The Alters, from This War of Mine's own studio, took a public beating in June 2025 for undisclosed AI text and translation and had to apologise (https://www.pcgamer.com/games/adventure/spitting-in-the-face-of-your-international-audience-the-alters-caught-using-generative-ai-for-background-text-and-translations-despite-not-disclosing-such-on-steam/, https://gameinformer.com/2025/06/30/11bit-studios-responds-to-the-alters-generative-ai-controversy). Clips of famous reciters and Dr Israr Ahmed lectures are copyrighted recordings, and Steam bans 'content you don't own or have adequate rights to'.
  fix: No ElevenLabs voice for Teta; use the friend or ney plus subtitles as the GDD already provides. If any generative music is used, disclose it plainly in the Steamworks survey and expect it to be a talking point in a game about real dead people. Use recitations only with a written licence or record your own. The code-generated buildings, kites, tatreez and MIDI are procedural, not generative AI, and need no disclosure; say so in the FAQ so nobody assumes otherwise.

- [major] Authorship: an Indian founder with no Palestinian collaborators yet is a marketing risk with the exact audience the plan counts on
  why: Coverage of both Liyla and Dreams on a Pillow is anchored in the developer being Palestinian, from Nablus (https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/1/18/what-is-dreams-on-a-pillow-the-palestinian-game-about-the-nakba). Palestinian Voices in Games, launched September 2025 with 50-plus industry professionals, exists 'to combat the underrepresentation and dehumanization of Palestinians' and to route support to Palestinian developers (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/console/game-professionals-form-new-coalition-to-combat-the-underrepresentation-and-dehumanization-of-palestinians-, https://aftermath.site/palestinian-voice-in-games-coalition-helps-game-developers/). That community will ask 'why you' on day one, and the GDD's own rule that readers' notes are binding cannot be met with zero readers.
  fix: Write to Palestinian Voices in Games now (https://buttondown.com/PalestinianVoicesInGames/archive) for readers and collaborators; credit Palestinian contributors above the fold on the store page and press kit; direct part of the charity share or a slice of revenue to Palestinian creators; say 'made with, not for' in every pitch. Hold the GDD's line that no public build ships before the readers sign off, which also means the demo date depends on finding them.

- [minor] Review bombing is a manageable risk, but the plan has no response prepared
  why: None of the comparables (Liyla, Path Out, Bury Me My Love, 1979 Revolution) has documented review bombing; the pressure came from Apple's categorisation, Iran's ban and the UK police, not Steam users. Valve's 2019 system detects anomalous off-topic review activity, removes those reviews from the score while leaving them visible, and notifies the developer; developers may opt out (https://kotaku.com/valve-says-it-will-remove-off-topic-review-bombs-from-s-1833332643, https://www.pcgamer.com/valve-takes-steps-against-steam-review-bombs/).
  fix: Keep store text about the street, not the state; do not opt out of the off-topic system; pre-write one calm paragraph for the community hub; watch the review histogram in launch week and report anomalies through Steamworks rather than arguing in threads.

- [minor] Dreams on a Pillow will be the press comparison and the plan does not mention it
  why: It is the most directly comparable Palestinian narrative game: 244,000 dollars from 3,300 LaunchGood backers after other platforms refused it, a 12-person team, targeting PC in 2027 (https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/2026/05/15/nakba-inspired-palestinian-game-dreams-on-a-pillow-enters-full-production/, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_on_a_Pillow). SUMUD would arrive first and every article will compare them.
  fix: Position SUMUD as the present-day companion to a 1948 story rather than a competitor; study its Steam page and coverage as prior art in the GDD, as the document already promises; approach Rasheed Abueideh only with something to offer, such as the kite campaign as a shared ending.

## Missed opportunities
- The itch.io charity-bundle ecosystem is the one proven channel to this exact audience: the 2021 Palestinian Aid bundle raised 902,365 dollars in a week with Liyla as its anchor, Games for Gaza (2023) 365,554 dollars, the Palestinian Relief Bundle (2024) 578,573 dollars (https://www.pcgamer.com/itchios-palestinian-relief-bundle-wraps-up-at-dollar900000/, https://kotaku.com/games-for-gaza-itchio-israel-palestine-charity-1850968071, https://itch.io/b/2321/palestinian-relief-bundle), and Palestinian Voices in Games runs a Games for Palestine bundle (https://buttondown.com/PalestinianVoicesInGames/archive/palestinian-voices-in-games-games-for-palestine/). Get the demo, soundtrack and kite patterns into the next one.
- LaunchGood, the Muslim crowdfunding platform that accepted Dreams on a Pillow after others refused and delivered 244,000 dollars from 3,300 backers (https://www.pcgamer.com/games/palestinian-developer-raises-more-than-usd200-000-to-make-dreams-on-a-pillow-a-game-about-the-horrors-of-the-1948-nakba/), fits the founder's halal positioning and could fund the singer, the readers and the moderation.
- A charity supporter DLC with three price tiers and identical content, the War Child pattern that has been live on Steam since 2015 (https://store.steampowered.com/app/348040/This_War_of_Mine_War_Child_Charity/), is the cleanest 'buy at full price and give more' mechanism and keeps the base price honest.
- A Steam Playtest as the pre-launch funnel: it needs no demo, appears as a sign-up on the main page and leaves wishlists untouched (https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest). Use it for the five-stranger playtests already in the production plan.
- The sources tab is a press asset and, later, the documentary/artistic argument under Google Play's Sensitive Events exception; ship it in the demo and put it on the store page.
- The classroom route: This War of Mine was added to Poland's school reading list (https://kotaku.com/this-war-of-mine-added-to-polands-high-school-reading-l-1844086680). A one-page teacher's guide built from the sources tab is cheap and gives outlets a second story.
- The halal audio constraint is also a positioning: a Steam narrative game with nasheed and no instruments is rare and reaches a large audience the plan's localisation list (Turkish, Indonesian, Malay, Urdu) already points at. Say it out loud instead of treating it as a limitation.
- Time-limited pledges are proven and low-risk: 11 bit donated all This War of Mine profits for seven days in 2022 and raised 850,000 dollars (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_War_of_Mine). A launch-week 100 percent pledge on top of the standing fixed share gives the founder the '100%' headline without the permanent tax problem.

## Keep
- Steam first on Windows, macOS and Linux, with mobile deliberately last; the Liyla precedent checks out (Apple rejected it as 'not appropriate in the games category' in May 2016 and reversed within a week: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liyla_and_the_Shadows_of_War) and the current Apple 1.1.2/1.1.7 and Google Play Sensitive Events rules make the caution stronger than the GDD states.
- The GDD's charity wording, 'a fixed share of net revenue to a named medical charity, stated on the store page', over the founder's 100 percent; only make the share, the definition of net and the charity name explicit.
- The kite campaign as a feature that is also marketing; it is the one element that gives press a story beyond 'a Gaza game' and gives every supporter a reason to share. Keep it, add moderation.
- Free demo of Day 1 with an itch.io mirror, demo keys to narrative streamers a week early, Discord from the first public build, and weekly generator-in-action posts; these match the zero-following playbook of festivals plus direct creator outreach.
- Accessibility as launch requirements, including the separate rumble bus and the photosensitivity mode; they are cheap, reviewable and often cited by outlets.
- The press angle 'not a game about a war, a game about a street that refuses to stop being a street' and opening every preview build on the kite festival so the first screenshot is joy; this is exactly how Kotaku framed Dreams on a Pillow ('wants to show you a beautiful Palestine').
- English and Arabic at launch with the on-screen Arabic above the English; no microtransactions; a launch discount.
- Sensitivity readers binding on culture and dialect before any public build; this is now also the authorship defence and the gate for the demo date.
- Procedural, code-generated assets rather than generative AI; they need no Steam AI disclosure and sidestep the backlash that hit The Alters.
