## What the founder asked for, and what it means

"World class assets", with Silksong, Life is Strange and The Kite Runner in mind. What those three have that the first playable did not:

| Reference | What it has | What we take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silksong | Characters drawn and animated by hand with a strong, simple shape language; every movement has anticipation, weight and follow-through; controls that answer in one frame | Hand-made character motion; readable shapes; the feel spec in `docs/design/feel-and-readability.md` |
| Life is Strange | Acting: people who move and pause like people; lived-in rooms full of objects with a story; quiet moments | Performed motion; interiors dressed with the family's things; the sit spots |
| The Kite Runner | A child's-eye city at kite height; kites as colour and longing | The kite camera; kites as the purest colour in the world |

None of them is achieved by generated textures. The gap the founder felt ("dead, wooden, weird scarf, weird eyes, weird walk") is **motion and character design**, which is where the work goes.

## The rule on AI (changed 1 October 2026)

The founder allowed AI generation on 1 October ("ai generation allowed, go ahead make it good"). Generated images and sounds may ship, under lock F-09: never Quran; never music or anything imitating an instrument; never prompted on, trained on or traced from footage, photographs or likenesses of real Gazans or victims; every asset reviewed in-engine by the founder and culturally by the readers, with a `docs/rights.md` row naming the model and prompt; disclosed on Steam's AI survey and the store page. Reference boards may now include generated images, but the models must be told the style, never shown news footage.

**The route from here.** Images: Canva's image generation (the agent can call it) and Figma Weave models (Nano Banana for edits and consistent character sheets, Veo for motion reference; each Weave run costs the founder credits and needs the founder's approval in chat). The cloud container must be allowed to reach `*.canva.com` and `fal.media` to pull full files; until then only thumbnails arrive. Generated frames are keyed to silhouettes with `tools/roto.py` (classical keying), cleaned, and packed into `SpriteFrames` by the import tool; the pipeline below is otherwise unchanged.

## The look (locked, from the design document)

Layered silhouettes, near-black to deep indigo, lit by scarce warm light; colour lives in the sky, the sea, the haze, light spill and a few accents (kites, tatreez red, bread, fire, the wedding dress); tatreez as the language of the interface; the print grade (grain, dither, vignette, a slight ink wobble) so the frame reads as printed. Colour is a budget the days spend (`game/data/palette.json`). Not negotiable in production.

## Characters: the decision the lock must make

Every person on screen is a silhouette with one readable trait (the design document). The question is how their motion is made. Three methods; the lock runs a test and picks one.

| Method | How | Ceiling | Cost | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Code rig (today) | `game/scripts/figure.gd` draws bones every frame | Low: it is why the characters look wooden | Free | The agent |
| D. Generated frames (new, 1 Oct) | An image model draws Layla's model sheet, then each animation as a sprite sheet of silhouettes in the locked style; a video model can draw the motion first and the frames are keyed from it | High if frame-to-frame consistency holds; the test decides | Generation credits; clean-up hours | The agent, founder approves |
| B. Rotoscoped performance | Film real people performing each action against a plain wall; key the silhouette with classical (non-AI) keying; clean up by hand; play as frames | High: real weight, timing and gesture; this is how the lifelike motion of the early cinematic platformers was made | A phone, a tripod, a plain wall, costumes, performers' time; clean-up hours | The founder films; performers; the agent builds the keying and import tools; an artist or the founder cleans |
| C. Cut-out rig drawn by an artist | An artist draws each character as parts; the parts are rigged with Godot's Skeleton2D and animated by keyframes | High if the animator is skilled; puppet-like if not | An artist-animator's time | An artist |

**Recommendation (revised 1 Oct): D first, B as the fallback for any motion the models cannot keep consistent, an artist for clean-up and design when one joins.** Silhouettes need only the outline, which is exactly what rotoscoping captures, and it puts human performance into every character with no drawing budget. Real weight comes free: a performer carrying a real 20-litre jerrycan leans the way a child does. An artist later designs the costumes and shape language, cleans and stylises the frames, and draws what cannot be filmed.

**The animation test (lock week 1 to 2).** Layla's walk, run, jump and heavy carry, made up to four ways (D generated, A improved, B from phone footage, C if an artist is available), placed in the same beach scene, rendered and played on the founder's laptop. The founder picks by watching, not by reading. The test also settles the scarf and the face (below).

### Rotoscope pipeline (if B is chosen)

1. **Shot list.** Each character's animation list (below), with the props and costume for each.
2. **Filming.** Phone on a tripod at hip height, exactly side-on, 50 or 60 frames a second, a plain light wall, even light, the performer in the character's costume (a headscarf and long dress for Layla, a thobe and cane for Teta, a cap for Baba), real props. Consent in writing; minors only with a parent present and consenting. Women may be filmed by women; footage is private, never shared, and deleted once the silhouettes are made. No faces are needed.
3. **Keying.** `tools/roto.py` (to write): classical background difference and threshold (numpy and Pillow, no machine-learning segmentation), a clean-up pass, the frame reduced to a one-colour silhouette with a transparent background.
4. **Clean-up and style.** An artist or the founder fixes edges in Krita (free), simplifies, and exaggerates key poses a little; animation on twos (12 drawings a second) for a hand-made feel where it suits.
5. **Import.** Frames packed as sprite sheets for `AnimatedSprite2D` (`SpriteFrames`), 2 times display size (a 256-pixel-tall character on screen is drawn at about 512), with a per-frame hand position for carried items and the kite string. A Godot tool script builds the `SpriteFrames` resource from a folder so nothing is assembled by hand.
6. **Review.** Every animation is played in-engine in the pose-sheet scene (`game/tests/shot_figures.tscn`, to extend) and judged by the founder before it is used.

### Faces, eyes, the scarf

- **Eyes.** No glowing eye-lights by default: in a coloured world two yellow squares read as a mask (founder's note). Faces read by head angle, posture and a thin rim of light on the profile in close shots. The style frames confirm it.
- **Layla's scarf.** Designed on the model sheet with the readers' answer on custom and colour: a real headscarf's drape, filmed or drawn, not a simulated tail. In silhouette it is the same near-black as the body, lit by rim light; whether it carries a lighter value as her one accent is decided on the style frames, not in code. Her readable trait remains the kite and its string.
- **Modesty.** Women covered outside the home and before men who are not family; no tight clothing; the bride only in women's spaces; women's dancing never shown (the design document).

## The world

The world stays generated by code, because that part works (`docs/research/23-art-critique-1.md`: the skies, the grade and the home at dusk are at the bar). What production adds:

1. **Kill the flat ground** (critique item 1): a ground shader with a roughened edge, a wet band and foam on the beach, speckle, footprints behind Layla, and a foreground plane of occluders.
2. **Make lights light** (item 2): shadows with occluders on floors, flights and figures; a spill light per lit window; the candle and the oven as the brightest warm objects on their screens.
3. **Specific places** (items 4 to 6, 9): the one-sided coast with the harbour's breakwater and light, the painted boat on its keel with a net, the kiosk, the bakery front, cylindrical water tanks, satellite dishes, rebar, proper minaret profiles, laundry that moves in the wind, bougainvillea as one colour accent, the cat.
4. **Hero props.** The objects the story touches (the key, the kites, the jerrycan, the boat, the oven, the dress, the gate) are generated as silhouettes with transparent backgrounds in the locked style, cleaned, and replace the code-drawn versions; an artist redraws any that fail review. Everything stays swappable by path.
5. **The house as a real place** (Life is Strange's rooms): objects with a story on every shelf, each a possible look-at.

## Specifications

| --- | --- |
| Canvas | 1920 by 1080, 16:9; letterbox for cinematic beats; legible at 1280 by 800 (Steam Deck) |

| Rights | Every asset gets a row in `docs/rights.md` before it is merged; generated assets name the model and the prompt |
